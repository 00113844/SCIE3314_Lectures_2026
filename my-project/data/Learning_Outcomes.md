# SCIE3314 Overall Outcomes

- Understand the context of Australian agriculture in relation to world food production.
- Recall key characteristics of the major crop species grown in Western Australia.
- Know the major pathogens, insects and weeds that affect crops in Western Australia and how they can be best controlled
- Discuss how agricultural systems can be managed to achieve more sustainable production
and environmental outcomes.
- Demonstrate and articulate how models can simulate complex agricultural systems.
- Demonstrate critical thinking, particularly at a farming systems scale World, Australian and WA cropping
- Understand the place of Australian and WA crop production in relation to world production
and exports
- Recognise the key features of Mediterranean-type environments for grain production.
- Understand the timing of key crop management inputs in relation to seasonal changes in our
Mediterranean-type climate
- Understand why sheep production declined and cropping increased in the southern grain
producing areas of Australia
## Climate
- Understand the mechanisms and impact of key drivers of rainfall on WA cropping and the
Australian continent as a whole

## APSIM 
- Demonstrate use of and articulate how the APSIM model can be used to
improve nitrogen management of crops
- Understand how soil, crop, water and climate inputs are used in the model to predict
nitrogen availability
- Understand the limitations of crop models
Modelling
- Understand the uses and limitation of models in agriculture
- Understand the different types of models (descriptive vs explanatory, dynamic vs static,
spatial vs non spatial continuous vs discrete, stochastic vs deterministic etc.)
- Discuss how modelling can supplement experimentation
- Recall a common agricultural model and describe its use
Preparation for upcoming crop, rotations and no-tillage systems
- Recognise the need for accurate records in managing crops
- Understand how the various components of a cropping system must be considered when
preparing for an upcoming crop
- Understand the key factors affecting soil acidity and aluminium toxicity
- Understand the long-term effects and the economics of lime application
- Know how to interpret soil analyses
- Understand the effects of plant density and time of seeding on crop yield
- Recall the benefits and any disadvantages of rotations
- Discuss how farmers might successfully implement more diverse rotations in a wheat dominated system
- Understand the principles of conservation agriculture (no-tillage) and why farmers in
Western Australia have adopted this practice
- Demonstrate critical thinking about the components of successful no-tillage systems
- Recognise the benefits and disadvantages of tillage and how tillage can be applied into a ‘no-
tillage’ system in Western Australia
- Understand the key components of seeding machinery
- Discuss the differences (advantages and disadvantages) between tine and disc seeding systems
- Discuss the benefits of retaining crop stubble and why farmers sometimes reduce or remove
stubble Water use efficiency - WUE
- Gain an understanding of WUE in rainfed cropping systems of Mediterranean-type
environments
- Use WUE for comparison of wheat crop performance
- Understand the impact of agronomic practices and plant breeding on WUE
Cereal phenology and crop agronomy (legumes/pulses, lupin, wheat, barley, canola)
- Recall the grow stages of the main broadacre crops grown in Western Australia
- Understand the importance of growth stage in crop management
- Discuss the effects of Genotype, Environment and Management (G x E x M) and their
interactions on cereal phenology
- Understand how cereals compensate for stress (like drought or nutrient) at different stages
of crop growth.
- Discuss why cereal crops are less risky than legumes or canola for farmers in drier areas
- Understand the key wheat and barley grain quality characteristics and associated
classification
- Understand how agronomy, soils and climate can influence wheat and barley yield and grain
quality
- Recall the main pulses (grain legumes) grown in Australia and Western Australia
- Understand why pulses are important for human health and as rotation crops
- Discuss the major constraints to growing the different pulses in Western Australia
- Critically assess the interaction between pulse species and the growing environment
- Recall the main lupin species grown in Western Australia
- Understand the advantages and disadvantages of lupins
- Discuss the agronomy/management of lupins
- Discuss the key objectives of canola breeding programs and differences between types of
canola
- Discuss the agronomy/management of canola

## Spraying
- Appreciate that there are legal requirements for people recommending, applying, or storing
agrochemicals
- Recall the optimum droplet size of sprays for different targets
- Understand the implications of droplet size on spray efficacy and drift
- Understand how controlled droplet application (CDA) and hydraulic nozzles/sprayers work
- Discuss uses of CDA sprayers
- Recall the spray characteristics and uses of different hydraulic nozzles
- Discuss the implications of nozzles spacing and spray boom height on evenness of spray pattern
- Recall how nozzles are coded and rated
- Discuss the effect of weather conditions on spray efficacy
- Understand how to calibrate sprayers from first principles as well as using spray
charts/formulae
- Recall boom spray components
- Discuss the pros and cons of using pressure to change sprayer output 
- Understand how pulse width modulation (PWM) and other common spray systems work as well as their advantages and disadvantages
- Recall the main types of adjuvants
- Understand the uses and the issues with using adjuvants when spraying agrochemicals

## Weeds
- Understand the biology and life-cycle of our main crop weeds (annual ryegrass (Lolium rigidum) and wild radish (Raphanus raphanistrum))
- Discuss how these weeds can be managed by exploiting aspects of their life-cycle, including their relatively short seedbank life
- Discuss the concept of integrated weed management and how it can be used through the different stages of growing a crop to manage weeds
- Explain the importance of herbicides in our cropping systems
- Recall the characteristics of selective and non-selective herbicides
- Discuss how soil applied herbicides work and factors that affect their persistence in the soil
- Discuss how foliar herbicides work
- Discuss why herbicides are grouped by their mode of action (MOA)
- Understand the naming and labelling of herbicides
- Understand the differences between target site and metabolic resistance

## Crop diseases
- Recall the main canola diseases and understand why their importance is often underestimated
- Understand the importance of finding sources of resistance to these diseases, for breeding new cultivars (varieties)
- Understand why Australian breeding programs are not utilising available resistances and extension programs need to promote more cultivar options
- Recall the lifecycle of blackleg disease in canola and how the disease spreads
- Understand why blackleg is so severe in this Mediterranean environment
- Explain why blackleg disease cycles occur even when ascospore fallout is constrained to a brief period in the cropping season
- Understand why epidemics of blackleg initiated by conidia alone develop relatively slowly
- Recall the factors affecting disease epidemics
- Understand the impact of climate change on blackleg, Rhizoctonia and wheat rust losses in canola, cereals and wheat, respectively
- Understand why black spot in field pea and clover scorch disease in clover is expected to decrease with future climate change
- Know what are the most important fungal, nematode and virus disease of cereal, legume and Brassica crops
- Know the most common control option available and why they are effective
- Be able to recognise the different field and plant disease symptoms caused by Rhizoctonia
- Understand the importance of pathogenic variability (different pathogen strains/groups)
- Understand pathogen cycle
- Understand management options available for disease control and the difficulties faced with managing the disease
- Understand how suppressive soils work
- Understand different modes of action of fungicides
- Understand the limitations of using fungicide 

## Nematodes
- Recall the main plant parasitic nematodes in Western Australia
- Understand their lifecycles
- Discuss their diagnosis and management

## Precision agriculture
- Understand how machinery guidance systems work
- Describe how a controlled traffic system works
- Discuss the benefits of controlled traffic (tramline) systems and high accuracy guidance
- Describe situations where variable rate technology is most applicable in broadacre cropping systems
- Discuss how data from harvester yield monitors can be used
- Understand the principles of estimating crop biomass from reflectance of light (electromagnetic radiation)
- Describe how soil apparent electrical conductivity (ECa) may be used to map out different soil types in farmers paddocks
