# Crop Rotation Strategies and Cereal Phenology in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the trade-offs between crop diversity and financial profitability within Western Australian dryland rotations, emphasizing the reliability of cereals over broadleaf crops in water-limited environments. It further integrates the GxExM (Genotype $\times$ Environment $\times$ Management) framework and the Zadoks scale to optimize the precision timing of nitrogen and herbicide applications.

## Key Facts
- **Rotation Trade-offs**: "Maximum profit" rotations (wheat, barley, lupin, and fallow) generally outperform "maximum diversity" rotations in dry areas because wheat provides more reliable yields during drought than canola or legumes.
- **Cover Crop Impact**: While cover crops (e.g., vetch-oat) improve soil nitrogen and stubble, they can reduce short-term gross margins in dryland systems unless integrated with livestock for forage.
- **GxExM Framework**: Crop performance is determined by the interaction of Genotype (genetic makeup), Environment (climate and soil), and Management (farming practices).
- **Development Drivers**: Cereal growth is driven by thermal time (accumulated degree days), day length, and vernalization; spring wheats common in WA are less sensitive to day length than European winter wheats.
- **Zadoks Scale Application**: The Zadoks (GS) scale is essential for precision management; for example, 2,4-D amine herbicides must be applied after Z16 to prevent crop distortion.
- **Tillering and Nitrogen**: Nitrogen availability during the tillering stage (Z21–Z24) defines yield potential, but excessive tillering in dry seasons can lead to premature water depletion.

## Local Examples
- **Cunderdin Trial**: A 12-year long-term trial demonstrating that broadleaf crops are higher risk and are best grown following fallow periods to utilize stored soil moisture.
- **Yield Prophet**: A modeling tool used in WA to provide nitrogen recommendations based on soil analysis and environmental data.
- **Pasture Cropping**: An integration technique developed by Colin Seis involving the sowing of crops into dormant summer-active pastures.

## Relevant Crops
Wheat, Barley, Canola, Lupin, Vetch-Oats
