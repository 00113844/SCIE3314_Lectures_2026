# Lupin Production, Nutrition, and Management in Western Australia

## Summary
Lupins are critical legume components of Western Australian (WA) cropping systems, particularly valued for their ability to fix atmospheric nitrogen and their adaptation to acidic sandy soils. Effective management requires precise rhizobia inoculation, careful monitoring of manganese levels, and integrated weed control strategies to maintain yield and soil health within rotation sequences.

## Key Facts
- **Biological Nitrogen Fixation:** Lupins rely on symbiotic rhizobia bacteria to form root nodules for nitrogen fixation. In WA, these are applied via peat-based inoculants (requiring cool storage) or granular forms (e.g., Alaska) which use bentonite clay for stability. Inoculum persistence in the soil typically lasts 5–7 years.
- **Soil Adaptation:** Narrowleaf lupins (*Lupinus angustifolius*) are uniquely adapted to acidic sandy soils, whereas Albus lupins prefer slightly more alkaline conditions.
- **Nutritional Requirements:** Lupins generally do not require nitrogen fertilizer, as added N can inhibit rhizobia effectiveness. Manganese (Mn) is critical; deficiency causes seed splitting and is often exacerbated by liming, which reduces Mn availability.
- **Germination and Sowing:** Lupins exhibit epigeal germination (cotyledons emerge above ground). Standard seeding rates range from 100–120 kg/ha, typically utilizing a 25 cm row spacing to maintain consistency with wheat seeding equipment.
- **Weed Management Challenges:** Broadleaf weed control is a significant constraint. Management involves pre-emergent triazines, post-emergent Group A herbicides for grasses, and "crop topping" (late-season application of glyphosate or paraquat) to prevent weed seed set.
- **Rotational Benefits:** Traditional 1:1 wheat-lupin rotations provide a critical disease break and improve subsequent cereal yields through increased soil nitrogen.

## Local Examples
- **Uniwhite:** The first narrowleaf lupin variety developed by the Department of Agriculture, bred for disease resistance and yield potential.
- **Sandplain Lupin (*Lupinus consentiae*):** A native species that has become a problematic weed in narrowleaf lupin crops due to genetic similarity and seed longevity (up to 20 years).
- **Specialized Nutrition:** Products such as Summit’s Lupin Manganese and CSBP’s BigFos Manganese are specifically formulated for WA lupin requirements.
- **Alaska Granules:** A commercial rhizobia delivery system that allows for dry storage and mixing with fertilizers.
