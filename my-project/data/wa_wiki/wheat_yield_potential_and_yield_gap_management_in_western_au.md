# Wheat Yield Potential and Yield Gap Management in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the "yield gap"—the disparity between potential yield under ideal conditions and actual farm-gate yields—within Western Australian wheat systems. It emphasizes the critical role of water use efficiency (WUE) and the application of tactical and strategic management practices to optimize grain production in water-limited environments.

## Key Facts
- **Yield Gap Dynamics:** The yield gap is narrower in low-rainfall areas (<250 mm) where crops utilize most available water, and wider in high-rainfall areas where water losses are more significant.
- **Water Use Efficiency (WUE):** The standard WUE for wheat in WA is 20 kg of grain per hectare per mm of growing season rainfall; genetic improvements aim to increase this to 25 kg/ha/mm.
- **Rainfall Loss:** Approximately one-third (100–110 mm) of growing season rainfall is typically lost to evaporation and other non-productive sources.
- **Yield Variance Factors:** Environment (rainfall and temperature) is the primary driver of yield variance, followed by management (contributing up to 36%) and genotype.
- **Management Impact:** Approximately 70% of historical yield gains in WA are attributed to improved management practices rather than genetics alone.
- **Plant Population and Nitrogen:** Higher plant populations (e.g., 300 plants/m²) can reduce the nitrogen requirement to reach maximum yield due to increased inter-plant competition reducing individual head size.
- **Management Categories:** Tactical practices (e.g., seed rate, N application) provide immediate seasonal effects, while strategic practices (e.g., soil compaction and fertility management) offer long-term yield improvements.

## Local Examples
- **The "Lazy Agronomist" Approach:** A simplified estimation method developed by Wal Anderson calculating potential yield as: $\text{(Growing season rainfall} - \text{rainfall}/3) \times 20$.
- **Farmanko Consulting Data:** Analysis showing wide variance in farm efficiency, with some WA farms achieving 60–80% of their potential yield while others achieve only 30%.
- **Target Yield Benchmarks:** For a target yield of 4 t/ha, a seed rate of approximately 100 kg/ha is recommended to achieve 200 plants/m² (assuming 70% establishment).
