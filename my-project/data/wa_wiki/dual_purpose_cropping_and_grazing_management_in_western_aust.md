# Dual-Purpose Cropping and Grazing Management in Western Australia

## Summary
Dual-purpose cropping involves the strategic integration of livestock grazing with grain production to optimize both livestock weight gain and harvestable yield. In Western Australian systems, this requires precision management of sowing dates, grazing intensity, and phenological timing to mitigate yield penalties and livestock health risks.

## Key Facts
- **Sowing Requirements**: Early seeding (ideally by mid-May) is critical to establish sufficient biomass and maintain yield potential in grazed systems.
- **Phenological Constraints**: Grazing must ideally cease before the transition from tillering to stem elongation (Zadoks stage 3.0); grazing after this point damages developing heads and significantly reduces grain yield.
- **Grazing Intensity**: "Clip grazing" (light stocking) minimizes yield loss, whereas "crash grazing" (high stocking rates over small areas) increases the yield penalty, particularly if conducted late in the growth cycle.
- **Frost Mitigation**: Grazing delays crop phenology and flowering, which can be utilized as a strategic tool to protect crops in frost-prone regions.
- **Nutrient Management**: Nitrogen should be applied at seeding or post-grazing. Applying nitrogen immediately prior to grazing can lead to lethal nitrate toxicity in livestock.
- **Agronomic Risks**: Grazing can increase the incidence of blackleg in canola (especially in susceptible varieties) and may promote weed proliferation by increasing light penetration to the soil surface.
