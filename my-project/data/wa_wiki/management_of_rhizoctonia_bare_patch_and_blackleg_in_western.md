# Management of Rhizoctonia Bare Patch and Blackleg in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the epidemiology and management of two critical crop diseases in Western Australia: Rhizoctonia Bare Patch (a root disease) and Blackleg (a foliar disease). It highlights the interaction between pathogen life cycles, soil management practices—specifically the trade-offs of minimum tillage—and the environmental drivers of the WA Mediterranean climate.

## Key Facts
- **Rhizoctonia Bare Patch:** A single-cycle root disease that causes irregular, persistent patches and "pinched-off" spear-tipped roots, potentially reducing yields by up to 50%.
- **Tillage Interaction:** Minimum tillage can exacerbate Rhizoctonia by preserving hyphal networks; conversely, cultivation prior to sowing disrupts these networks and reduces inoculum.
- **Rhizoctonia Host Tolerance:** Tolerance varies significantly across cereals, with oats being the most tolerant and barley the least.
- **Pathogenic Strains:** AG8 is the most dominant and pathogenic Rhizoctonia strain in Australia; AG11 is a specialized strain targeting lupin hypocotyls in specific WA regions.
- **Blackleg Epidemiology:** A polycyclic foliar disease of canola caused by *Leptosphaeria maculans*, spreading via airborne ascospores (primary infection) and water-splashed conidia (secondary infection).
- **WA Pathogen Diversity:** Western Australia is a global hotspot for Blackleg evolution, originating 13 of the 26 known global races of *L. maculans*.
- **Environmental Drivers:** The WA climate favors Blackleg as dry summers preserve infested stubble, and early autumn rains synchronize spore release with crop germination.

## Local Examples
- **Geraldton, WA:** Region specifically affected by the AG11 strain of Rhizoctonia which targets lupin hypocotyls.
- **University of Western Australia (UWA):** Prof. Wallace Cowling has led the breeding of WA-specific resistant canola varieties to combat Blackleg.
- **Eyre Peninsula (2003):** A significant resistance breakdown of Blackleg that resulted in approximately $60 million in losses.
