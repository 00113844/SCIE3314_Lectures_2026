# Climate Impacts on Crop Disease Epidemiology in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the interaction between climatic variables and the prevalence of major crop diseases within Western Australian dryland systems. It analyzes how shifting temperature and rainfall patterns influence pathogen life cycles, presenting both increased risks for certain cereal rusts and strategic opportunities for adaptation in pulse and pasture production.

## Key Facts
- **Rhizoctonia Bare Patch:** Disease severity is positively correlated with low rainfall and conditions that impede rapid root growth (e.g., cold soil temperatures or late season breaks), which increases plant vulnerability.
- **Wheat Rust Dynamics:** Pathogen prevalence is temperature-dependent; while stripe rust favors cooler conditions (10–15°C), predicted temperature increases may reduce stripe rust outbreaks but increase the risk of catastrophic stem rust epidemics.
- **Pea Black Spot:** A major constraint on field pea expansion, this disease is driven by wind-borne ascospores from infested stubble. Future climate models suggest a shift toward earlier spore release, which may allow growers to sow earlier and avoid peak infection periods.
- **Clover Scorch:** This disease thrives in wet, cool conditions (17–24°C). A trend toward warmer, drier winter-spring conditions in WA has led to a significant decline in epidemics since the 1970s.
- **Residue Persistence:** Dry summers in southwestern WA facilitate the carryover of pathogens on crop residues. In WA, blackleg spores can persist on stubble for up to four years, significantly longer than in higher-rainfall regions like New South Wales.
- **Predictive Modeling:** Weather-based simulation tools are critical for separating climate-driven outcomes and informing practical farm-level decisions regarding sowing dates and variety selection.

## Local Examples
- **Regional Spore Variation:** Pea black spot ascospore release peaks mid-season in Geraldton, whereas southern regions like Skadden experience earlier peaks due to rainfall patterns.
- **Climate Projections:** In Esperance, Dalwallinu, Meriden, and Weijen, peak ascospore release for pea black spot is projected to occur approximately four weeks earlier under future climate scenarios.
- **Decision Support:** The Department of Agriculture’s "Blackleg Manager" is utilized as a primary tool for predicting disease timing and optimizing sowing windows.

## Relevant Crops
Wheat, Field Peas, Sub-clover
