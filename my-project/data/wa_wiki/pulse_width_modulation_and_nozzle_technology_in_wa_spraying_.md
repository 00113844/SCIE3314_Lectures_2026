# Pulse Width Modulation and Nozzle Technology in WA Spraying Systems

## Summary
This topic examines the application of Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) and advanced nozzle selection to optimize chemical delivery in Western Australian cropping systems. The focus is on maintaining droplet size consistency and spray uniformity while varying application rates to reduce drift and over-application.

## Key Facts
- **Pulse Width Modulation (PWM):** A precision technology that controls spray rates via a "duty cycle" (the proportion of time a nozzle is open), managed by solenoids. Unlike traditional systems, PWM varies output without changing spray pressure.
- **Droplet Consistency:** By maintaining constant pressure, PWM ensures a consistent droplet size regardless of the application rate, which is critical for managing drift and efficacy.
- **Striping and Overlap:** Uneven coverage (striping) can occur at low duty cycles or with narrow fan angles. This is mitigated by increasing boom height for 100% overlap or utilizing wider angle nozzles (e.g., 110°).
- **Nozzle Compatibility:** PWM systems can cause pressure fluctuations that make Air Induction (AI) nozzles prone to "skipping." Consequently, pre-orifice low-drift nozzles are often preferred in PWM setups.
- **Turn Compensation:** PWM allows for individual nozzle control, automatically increasing the duty cycle on the outer edges of the boom during turns to compensate for higher ground speed.
- **Operational Buffers:** To maintain flexibility in speed and rate, it is recommended that growers operate at 60–80% of the maximum speed (100% duty cycle).
- **Adjuvant Integration:** The use of surfactants (to reduce surface tension), oils (to enhance penetration), and acidifiers (to prevent pesticide hydrolysis) is essential for optimizing the chemical-target interaction.

## Local Examples
- **Southern Cross:** A reported instance of striping occurring with high-output coarse nozzles operating at low duty cycles.
- **2,4-D Regulations:** WA summer application mandates for 2,4-D require the use of low-drift formulations and coarse droplet nozzles to minimize environmental risk.
