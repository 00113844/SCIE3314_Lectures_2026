# Herbicide Tolerance Systems in WA Broadacre Cropping

## Overview
Herbicide tolerance (HT) in Western Australian cropping refers to the use of crop cultivars genetically engineered or bred to withstand specific herbicide chemistries that would otherwise be lethal to the crop. These systems are fundamental to precision weed management, allowing farmers to apply targeted chemical controls to reduce weed competition and manage herbicide-resistant species within diverse crop rotations.

## Key Themes
- **Diversification of HT Traits**: WA agriculture employs a broad spectrum of tolerance technologies to manage different weed profiles, including Roundup Ready (glyphosate), Clearfield (ALS inhibitors/imidazolinones), Triazine tolerance (TT), and Liberty Link (glufosinate ammonium).
- **Crop-Specific Implementation**: The application of HT traits varies by crop; while canola utilizes a wide array of options (RR, TT, CL, Liberty Link), barley specifically leverages Clearfield (CL) varieties to enable the use of Group B ME herbicides.
- **Targeted Weed Control**: Specific HT traits are selected based on the dominant weed pressure; for example, Clearfield canola is strategically utilized for its efficacy against brome and barley grass.
- **Integration with Break Crop Strategy**: HT varieties are critical components of "break crops" (such as canola and lupins), enabling the use of herbicides that are incompatible with cereals, thereby breaking the cycle of weed infestation.
- **Management Limitations**: Despite the availability of HT traits, certain crops like lupins remain challenging for broadleaf weed control, often requiring the integration of pre-emergent herbicides and specific seeding equipment (e.g., tine seeders) for soil incorporation.
- **Economic Trade-offs**: The choice of HT variety often intersects with cultivar type; for instance, the higher cost of hybrid canola is weighed against its increased vigor and yield potential compared to open-pollinated (OP) varieties.

## Regional Patterns
The adoption of HT technologies is consistent across the WA wheatbelt, though cultivar selection varies by zone, with hybrid HT canola dominating high-yield regions like the South Coast, while OP varieties are more common in the higher-risk Eastern Wheatbelt.
