# Herbicide Modes of Action and Resistance Management in Western Australia

## Summary
Herbicides are classified by their Mode of Action (MOA), which defines the specific biochemical process they disrupt to eliminate target weed species. In Western Australian broadacre systems, understanding MOA is critical for implementing rotation strategies to delay the development of herbicide resistance and ensuring crop selectivity.

## Key Facts
- **MOA Classifications**: Major groups include Group 1 (ACCase inhibitors targeting fatty acid synthesis), Group 2 (ALS inhibitors targeting amino acid synthesis), and Group 9 (EPSPS inhibitors, e.g., glyphosate).
- **Resistance Mechanisms**: 
    - **Target-site resistance**: An alteration in the herbicide binding site on an enzyme, common in Group 1 and 2 herbicides.
    - **Metabolic (non-target-site) resistance**: The plant detoxifies the chemical or overproduces the target enzyme to survive exposure.
- **Cross-Resistance**: Occurs when a weed develops resistance to one herbicide that confers resistance to others, either within the same MOA group or across different groups; this is prevalent in annual ryegrass and wild oats.
- **Selectivity and Application**: Herbicides are categorized by their target (leaves vs. roots), selectivity (grasses vs. broadleaf), and movement (contact vs. translocated).
- **Residual Management**: Certain herbicides, such as Group 15 (pyroxasulfone), possess soil residual activity requiring strict adherence to recropping intervals to avoid damaging subsequent crops in a rotation.
- **Professional Liability**: Agronomists are legally and ethically responsible for ensuring herbicide recommendations align with label instructions to prevent crop damage and residue issues in harvested grain.

## Local Examples
- **AHRI (University of Western Australia)**: The Australian Herbicide Resistance Initiative provides essential screening services to test suspected resistant weed populations.
- **Sakura (Pyroxasulfone)**: A Group 15 pre-emergent herbicide used in WA for controlling annual ryegrass and barley grass in wheat, lupins, and chickpeas.
- **Crop-Specific Selectivity**: Diclofop-methyl is used for selective post-emergence control of grassy weeds in wheat and barley, whereas Fluazifop-P-butyl is reserved for broadleaf crops like canola and lupins.
