# Spray Drift Management and Application Technology

## Overview
Spray drift management in Western Australian broadacre systems is the strategic integration of nozzle physics, environmental monitoring, and precision rate control to maximize chemical efficacy while minimizing off-target movement. Given the scale of WA paddocks and the volatility of certain herbicides, this concept focuses on maintaining a precise droplet spectrum to balance target coverage with environmental stewardship.

## Key Themes
- **Droplet Dynamics and VMD**: Application efficacy is quantified by the Volume Median Diameter (VMD). There is an inverse correlation between spray pressure and droplet size; while higher pressure improves coverage for specific targets (e.g., canola budworm), it increases the proportion of fine droplets, significantly elevating drift risk.
- **Nozzle Engineering and Selection**: Hydraulic nozzles, particularly the 110-degree flat fan, are the broadacre standard. To mitigate drift, Air Induction (AI) nozzles are employed to incorporate air bubbles into droplets, increasing their size and reducing the drift-prone fraction. For ultra-low volume (ULV) applications (<5 L/ha), Controlled Droplet Application (CDA) utilizes centrifugal discs to produce highly uniform droplets.
- **Environmental Thresholds and Atmospheric Risks**: Drift is managed by monitoring Delta T (the difference between dry and wet bulb temperatures), with a threshold of <8°C recommended to prevent droplet evaporation. Additionally, operators must avoid temperature inversions—where cold air is trapped beneath warm air—as these conditions cause droplets to remain suspended and drift over long distances.
- **PWM vs. Pressure-Based Rate Control**: Traditional pressure-based systems alter droplet size when travel speed changes, increasing drift risk at higher speeds. Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) decouples rate from pressure by switching nozzles on and off at high frequencies (10–100 Hz). This maintains a constant droplet spectrum regardless of speed, utilizing a "duty cycle" to manage the application rate.
- **Chemical Volatility and Target Sensitivity**: High-risk chemicals, specifically the hormone disruptor 2,4-D, require strict adherence to coarse spray quality to protect sensitive targets such as vineyards. This necessitates a hierarchical planning approach that integrates farm-level sensitive area mapping with paddock-level nozzle selection.
- **Regulatory Compliance and Monitoring**: Legal requirements in WA mandate precise weather recording at the start, during, and end of operations, with wind speed measured at 2 metres and temperature/humidity measured at 1.25 metres (shaded).

## Regional Patterns
WA broadacre systems have largely transitioned away from shielded sprayers due to the impracticality of slow operational speeds over vast areas, favoring high-speed PWM systems and GRDC-standardized nozzle selection guides.
