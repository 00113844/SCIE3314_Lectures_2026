# Pesticide Application and Droplet Size Management

## Overview
Pesticide application in Western Australian broadacre systems is defined by the technical optimization of droplet size to balance target coverage with the mitigation of spray drift. This management is critical for ensuring chemical efficacy—particularly for contact-based pesticides—while adhering to strict environmental regulations and protecting non-target crops.

## Key Themes
- **The Coverage-Drift Trade-off**: There is an inverse relationship between droplet size and coverage. Smaller droplets (e.g., 100 $\mu$m) provide exponentially higher coverage per milliliter than larger droplets (e.g., 1 mm), but they are significantly more susceptible to wind-induced drift.
- **Quantifying Spray Quality**: Spray performance is measured using two primary metrics: Volume Median Diameter (VMD), which bisects the total spray volume, and Number Median Diameter (NMD), which bisects the total number of droplets.
- **Droplet Classification and Strategy**:
    - **Coarse (>300 $\mu$m)**: Low drift risk; utilized primarily for soil-acting herbicides.
    - **Medium (200–300 $\mu$m)**: A versatile balance of penetration and drift control; standard for most foliar herbicides and fungicides.
    - **Fine (<200 $\mu$m)**: Maximum canopy penetration and coverage; preferred for foliar insecticides and fungicides, despite high drift risk.
- **Nozzle Technology and Mechanics**: Application relies on hydraulic nozzles where flow rate is proportional to the square root of the pressure. Air Induction (AI) nozzles are employed to reduce drift by incorporating air bubbles that shatter upon impact to maintain coverage.
- **Precision Boom Geometry**: To ensure 100% overlap and uniform application with standard 50 cm nozzle spacing, boom height must be maintained between 50–80 cm above the target. Modern systems integrate sonic sensors and hydraulic actuators for real-time height compensation.
- **Safety and Regulatory Compliance**: Technical controls for operator safety include the use of tractor cabs with filtered air. In Western Australia, legal compliance is managed through the mandatory OzChem training program.

## Regional Patterns
In Western Australia, the high potential for drift from herbicides such as 2,4-D creates significant tension in mixed-cropping regions, where broadacre applications can cause severe off-target damage to neighboring vineyards.
