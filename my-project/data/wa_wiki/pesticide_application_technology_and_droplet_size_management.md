# Pesticide Application Technology and Droplet Size Management in WA

## Summary
Effective pesticide application in Western Australian broadacre systems relies on the precise management of droplet size to balance target coverage with the risk of spray drift. The integration of advanced nozzle technology and real-time boom height compensation is essential for maintaining uniform application across variable terrain and protecting non-target crops.

## Key Facts
- **Droplet Size Metrics**: Spray performance is quantified using Volume Median Diameter (VMD), which divides spray volume into two equal halves, and Number Median Diameter (NMD), which divides the total number of droplets in half.
- **Droplet Categories**: 
    - **Coarse (>300 $\mu$m)**: Low drift; ideal for soil-acting herbicides.
    - **Medium (200–300 $\mu$m)**: Balanced coverage and penetration; used for foliar herbicides, insecticides, and fungicides.
    - **Fine (100–200 $\mu$m)**: High coverage but high drift risk; used for foliar insect targets.
    - **Mists/Aerosols (25–200 $\mu$m)**: Remain airborne longer; used for flying insects.
- **Nozzle Mechanics**: Hydraulic nozzles are the most common; their flow rate is proportional to the square root of the pressure (doubling flow requires a fourfold increase in pressure).
- **Drift Mitigation**: Air Induction (AI) nozzles reduce drift by incorporating air bubbles into the droplet, which then shatter upon impact to provide effective coverage.
- **Boom Geometry**: Optimal boom height is 50–80 cm above the target. With standard 50 cm nozzle spacing, this height ensures the 100% overlap required for consistent application.
- **Precision Integration**: High-end sprayers utilize sonic sensors and hydraulic actuators for automatic height compensation and spot-spraying cameras to optimize chemical use.

## Local Examples
- **Disease Monitoring**: The WA Department of Agriculture provides weekly updates on powdery mildew and insect pressures within the wheat belt.
- **Off-Target Damage**: The high drift potential of herbicides like 2,4-D poses a significant risk to neighboring vineyards in mixed-cropping regions.
- **Operator Safety**: Adoption of tractor cabs with specialized filtration systems and chemical metering systems to reduce direct pesticide exposure for WA farmers.
