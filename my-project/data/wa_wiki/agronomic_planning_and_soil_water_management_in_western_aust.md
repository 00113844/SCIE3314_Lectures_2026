# Agronomic Planning and Soil-Water Management in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic covers the integration of soil analysis, water availability metrics, and chemical resistance management to optimize crop selection and yield targets in Western Australian (WA) broadacre systems. It emphasizes the use of Growing Season Rainfall (GSR) and soil physical properties to determine yield potential and the application of precision agriculture to manage spatial variability across different soil types.

## Key Facts
- **Growing Season Rainfall (GSR):** In southern WA, GSR is calculated as total rainfall from April to October minus estimated evaporation (approximately 110 mm for wheat), representing the available water for crop growth.
- **Yield Potential:** A practical benchmark for wheat and barley in WA is approximately 20 kg of grain produced per mm of available water. Canola and pulses generally exhibit lower kg/mm efficiency due to higher energy requirements (oil content) or different conversion efficiencies.
- **Soil pH and Crop Suitability:** 
    - **Acidic Soils:** Narrow-leaf lupins and oats are tolerant of low pH; wheat performance declines significantly below pH 4.5.
    - **Alkaline Soils:** Barley, chickpeas, and lentils prefer heavier, more alkaline soils.
- **Aluminum (Al) Toxicity:** A critical constraint in acidic soils, typically becoming toxic below pH 4.7. Toxicity is measured via 1M KCl or 0.02M CaCl2 extraction, with levels >50 mg/kg (KCl) considered very high.
- **Water Holding Capacity (WHC):** Soil texture dictates water availability; sandy soils hold 7–10% volumetric water, while heavy clays can hold up to 40%. However, clays hold water more tightly, meaning loamy soils often provide the highest available water content.
- **Soil Water Measurement:** Water content is measured gravimetrically (g water/100 g soil) or volumetrically (cm³/100 cm³ soil). Conversion between the two requires the soil's bulk density, measured via core sampling.
- **Chemical Resistance:** Resistance to Group 1 (e.g., haloxyfop) and Group 2 (ALS inhibitors) herbicides is a major constraint in WA, necessitating strict rotation of modes of action.

## Local Examples
- **Predictor B:** A SARDI soil DNA testing service used in WA to proactively assess risks from pathogens and nematodes (e.g., Rhizoctonia, take-all, cereal cyst nematode).
- **IRI:** A provider of herbicide resistance testing that grows weed seeds to assess efficacy against various chemical groups.
- **CSBP:** A primary provider of soil testing and nutrient analysis services for WA growers.
- **GRDC Grow Notes:** The standard research-based resource for regional crop management in wheat, canola, and barley.
