# Controlled Traffic Farming (CTF) Systems in Western Australia

## Summary
Controlled Traffic Farming (CTF) is a precision agriculture strategy that permanently separates machinery traffic lanes from crop production zones to eliminate soil compaction. By confining heavy machinery to designated tracks, CTF preserves the soil structure in crop zones, significantly increasing plant-available water (PAW) and enhancing root penetration in Western Australian dryland systems.

## Key Facts
- **Soil Compaction Mitigation:** Standard farming operations can compact over 50% of a paddock; CTF eliminates this by restricting traffic, preventing the formation of dense soil layers typically found between 10 and 40 cm deep.
- **Hydrological Benefits:** CTF can increase plant-available water by up to 75 mm, which is critical for yield stability during moisture-limited seasons.
- **Operational Efficiency:** Driving on consolidated tracks can reduce fuel consumption by up to 50% and reduce machinery overlap, potentially lowering operational costs by 10%.
- **Technical Requirements:** Implementation requires high-precision guidance systems with 2 cm accuracy to ensure machinery remains strictly on designated tracks.
- **Residue Management Challenges:** Uneven stubble distribution from wide harvester fronts can lead to localized accumulations of organic carbon and nutrients (e.g., potassium) directly behind the harvester, potentially causing crop waviness over time.
- **Seeding Technology:** While disc seeders are superior for high-residue environments (common in South America), they have low adoption in the WA wheat belt (~1%) due to the versatility of tine seeders across diverse soil types and their superior herbicide incorporation.
- **Rehabilitation:** Existing compacted soils can be rehabilitated through a one-off deep rip followed by the integration of deep-rooted biological breakers such as tillage radish, corn, sorghum, or safflower.

## Local Examples
- **Wimmera Region:** A grower reported an increase of 50 mm of plant-available water after integrating no-till practices with CTF.
- **Cunderden Trials:** Long-term (12-year) research demonstrated significantly higher soil organic carbon levels in the zones directly behind harvesters compared to the outer edges of the paddock.
- **WA Wheatbelt:** Adoption of disc seeders remains very low (approximately 1%) compared to the preference for tine seeders.
