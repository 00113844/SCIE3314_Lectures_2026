# Spray Planning and Precision Rate Control Systems in Western Australia

## Summary
Effective chemical application in Western Australian broad-acre systems requires a multi-tiered planning approach, integrating farm-level chemical rotations with paddock-level mixing and nozzle specifications. The transition from traditional pressure-based rate control to Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) allows for precise application rates and constant droplet size, significantly reducing environmental risk and improving input efficiency.

## Key Facts
- **Strategic Planning:** Comprehensive spray management involves detailed mixing plans (specifying order and loading) and rigorous record-keeping to evaluate long-term management efficacy.
- **2,4-D Risk Management:** Due to high volatility and the risk of offsite movement affecting sensitive crops (e.g., vineyards), 2,4-D applications require coarse spray quality, often achieved through Air Induction (AI) nozzles.
- **Weather Compliance:** Legal requirements in WA necessitate measuring wind speed at 2 metres above ground, and temperature/humidity at 1.25 metres (shaded), with recordings taken at the start, during, and end of operations.
- **Pressure-Based vs. PWM Control:** Traditional pressure-based systems alter droplet size when speed changes (increasing drift risk at higher speeds), whereas PWM maintains constant pressure by switching nozzles on and off at high frequencies (10–100 Hz).
- **PWM Duty Cycle:** Application rates in PWM systems are controlled by the "duty cycle" (the proportion of time a nozzle is open); for example, a 75% duty cycle delivers 75% of the maximum flow without altering the droplet spectrum.
- **Precision Advantages:** PWM enables individual nozzle control, which eliminates over-application during headland turns and allows for significant speed flexibility (e.g., 23 km/h down to 6 km/h) without compromising spray quality.

## Local Examples
- **Sensitive Targets:** Vineyards are highlighted as high-risk areas for 2,4-D drift in WA.
- **Commercial Systems:** Implementation of PWM technology is seen in systems such as Case IH AIM Command (alternating nozzles) and John Deere Exact Apply (dual solenoids).
