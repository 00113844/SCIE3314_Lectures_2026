# Barley and Wheat Agronomy and Protein Management in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the physiological drivers of yield and grain protein in Western Australian (WA) cereals, specifically focusing on the timing of nitrogen uptake and the influence of soil moisture. Precision management of these variables is essential for aligning crop quality with strict market classifications and optimizing the trade-off between yield and protein concentration.

## Key Facts
- **Nitrogen Dynamics:** Nitrogen (N) demand peaks during stem elongation; deficiency during this phase can cause tiller loss and significantly reduce yield potential.
- **Protein Remobilization:** Approximately 75% of the nitrogen accumulated in shoots is remobilized to the grain during maturity, though late-season N application can boost protein if moisture is available.
- **Moisture Trade-off:** Sufficient soil moisture during grain filling increases starch accumulation and overall yield but can dilute protein concentration.
- **Barley Morphology:** Two-row barley is the dominant type grown in WA, characterized by only the central floret being fertile at each node.
- **Disease Vectors:** Barley grass (*Hordeum glaucum*) serves as a critical reservoir for stubble-borne diseases, such as net blotch, posing a risk to crop health.
- **Genetic Complexity:** Bread wheat is a hexaploid species, making its breeding and genetic management more complex than the diploid structure of barley.

## Local Examples
- **APWN (Australian Premium Noodle Wheat):** A specific wheat classification used in WA for high-value export markets for white salted and instant noodles.
- **DPIRD Crop Sowing Guide:** The primary regional resource for selecting varieties based on herbicide tolerance and protein characteristics.
- **Two-row Barley:** The preferred variety type in WA to meet specific malting and feed market requirements.
