# Introduction to Agricultural Pests and Integrated Pest Management in WA

## Summary
This topic explores the biological and ecological foundations of agricultural pests, defining them through an anthropocentric lens as organisms that harm human interests, specifically crop and livestock production. In the context of Western Australian agriculture, understanding pest metamorphosis, dietary breadth (polyphagy vs. oligophagy), and the critical role of quarantine is essential for implementing effective integrated pest management (IPM) strategies.

## Key Facts
- **Pest Classification**: Pests are categorized by their dietary range: monophagous (single species), oligophagous (few species within a family), and polyphagous (broad diet across multiple families).
- **Arthropod Dominance**: Arthropods constitute the majority of agricultural pests, with insects representing approximately 90% of terrestrial arthropods.
- **Metamorphosis Types**: Insect pests are classified by their development: ametabolous (no change), hemimetabolous (partial change, e.g., grasshoppers), and holometabolous (complete metamorphosis, e.g., caterpillars/butterflies).
- **Quarantine Importance**: Australia's isolated geography necessitates strict quarantine to prevent the introduction of devastating pests, such as the Colorado potato beetle or citrus canker.
- **Biological Control**: Beneficial insects, such as holometabolous Neuropterans (lacewings), serve as natural predators for sap-sucking pests like aphids, reducing reliance on chemical interventions.

## Local Examples
- **Red-legged Earth Mites**: Significant pests in WA pastures and canola crops; they suck photosynthetic cell contents, resulting in characteristic "silvering" of the foliage.
- **Slaters (Woodlice)**: Land-adapted crustaceans that attack emergent seedlings in southern Western Australia, particularly following rainfall events.
- **Fall Armyworm**: A recently introduced pest in Kununurra (northern Australia) that poses a severe threat to maize production due to its high mobility and polyphagous nature.

## Relevant Crops
Canola, Maize, Wheat, Oats, Barley
