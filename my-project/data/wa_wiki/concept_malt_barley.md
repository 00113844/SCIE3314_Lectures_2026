# Quality and Protein Management of Malt Barley

## Overview
Malt barley production in Western Australia is defined by a strict G x E x M (Genotype x Environment x Management) interaction aimed at meeting precise brewing specifications. Unlike feed barley, malt barley must adhere to narrow protein windows and plumpness metrics to avoid downgrading, requiring precision management of nitrogen (N) and water during critical growth stages.

## Key Themes
- **The Protein Window:** To ensure optimal brewery filtration and beer head duration, grain protein must typically fall between 9% and 12.5%. Protein levels exceeding this threshold impair filtration and cause haze, while levels below 9.5% reduce fermentation efficiency and foam stability.
- **The Dilution Effect:** There is an inverse relationship between grain yield and protein concentration; as yield increases, a fixed amount of available nitrogen is distributed across a larger volume of grain, effectively "diluting" the protein percentage.
- **Nitrogen Dynamics and Remobilization:** Nitrogen uptake occurs in two phases: initial vegetative accumulation and subsequent remobilization to the grain during filling (approximately 75–90% of shoot N). While the total rate of N application primarily drives protein levels, applying N at stem elongation (Z31) can increase the proportion of grain achieving the premium Malt 1 grade.
- **Plumpness and Screenings:** Grain quality is quantified by test weight and screenings (percentage of grains passing through a 2mm or 2.2mm slotted sieve). Malt 1 grade requires $\le 7\%$ screenings; high screenings are typically induced by water stress during grain fill, powdery mildew, or early weed competition.
- **Environmental Quality Degradation:** Pre-harvest stress, particularly late-season rains, can trigger grain germination, increasing $\alpha$-amylase activity and lowering the "falling number." Additionally, high humidity and light showers can reduce grain brightness (target $>59$ lux), leading to yellowing or graying.
- **Precision Decision Support:** Management of the protein-yield trade-off is supported by modeling tools such as Yield Prophet, SYN (Select Your Nitrogen), and Pi Cal, which account for soil organic carbon thresholds and nitrogen carryover from legume rotations.

## Regional Patterns
WA exclusively utilizes two-rowed spring barley, and regional variations in humidity—particularly in the South Coast—make grain brightness a more critical management constraint in those areas compared to drier inland zones.
