# Integrated Stored Grain Pest and Biosecurity Management

## Overview
Stored grain management in Western Australia is a high-stakes biosecurity operation designed to maintain "nil tolerance" standards for live insects to protect international export markets. This system integrates sealed bulk storage infrastructure, chemical fumigation—primarily using phosphine—and precision monitoring to mitigate the economic risks posed by both endemic pests and exotic threats.

## Key Themes
- **Export-Driven Biosecurity Framework:** Because approximately 90% of WA grain is exported, the state adheres to strict federal mandates for insect-free grain. The introduction of exotic pests, such as the Capra beetle, represents a systemic risk that could potentially eliminate 50% of available grain markets.
- **Phosphine Resistance Dynamics:** While phosphine remains the primary control agent due to its penetration and low residue, resistance is increasing by roughly 5% annually. In WA, the rust red flour beetle exhibits a high frequency of resistance (~80%), though it is generally categorized as "weak" (20–40x normal dose) compared to the "strong" resistance (up to 600x) observed in eastern Australian states.
- **Precision Monitoring and Diagnostics:** Management is shifting from calendar-based treatments to data-driven interventions. This is supported by a national database of over 80,000 GPS-tagged resistance tests and the implementation of rapid, same-day diagnostic tests for growers.
- **Infrastructure and Application Optimization:** The transition from bag storage to sealed bulk systems is critical for fumigant efficacy. The use of thermosiphons improves air circulation and gas distribution, while FOSS cards provide simplified application instructions and copper-strip indicators to verify gas exposure.
- **Ecological Persistence and Reinfestation:** Pest management extends beyond the silo, as research indicates that primary pests like the lesser grain borer and rust red flour beetle can survive in native bushland, creating a permanent reservoir for reinfestation.
- **High-Intensity Eradication Protocols:** For critical biosecurity breaches or exotic infestations, WA employs aggressive eradication strategies, including methyl bromide fumigation and the airtight "shrink-wrapping" of infested structures.

## Regional Patterns
WA maintains a more stringent legislative and management framework than eastern states, resulting in a lower intensity of insecticide resistance ("weak" vs "strong"). However, the state's heavy reliance on export markets makes it disproportionately vulnerable to exotic pest introductions.
