# No-Till Farming Systems and Conservation Agriculture in Western Australia

## Summary
No-till farming in Western Australia is a comprehensive conservation system characterized by minimal soil disturbance, stubble retention, and controlled traffic to maximize water-use efficiency. With a 98% adoption rate among growers, the system is essential for maintaining productivity in moisture-limited environments with fragile, degraded soils.

## Key Facts
- **Water Conservation:** Tillage can result in the loss of 15–20 mm of rainfall per soil turn through evaporation, making no-till critical for moisture-limited regions.
- **Stubble Dynamics:** Surface residue protects soil from raindrop impact, maintains biopores for infiltration, cools the soil surface, and provides organic matter for soil biota.
- **Seeding Technology:** Tine seeders (5–20% disturbance) are common in WA for their ability to incorporate herbicides like trifluralin, whereas disc seeders (zero disturbance) offer lower fuel consumption (2 L/ha vs 5 L/ha) and superior stubble handling.
- **Controlled Traffic:** The use of RTK GPS allows for precision within 2 cm, confining soil compaction to narrow strips to preserve soil tilth and improve fuel efficiency.
- **Tactical Tillage:** While generally avoided, tillage is used strategically for deep ripping (every 6–8 years in sandy soils) to alleviate compaction or moldboard ploughing to reset herbicide-resistant weed cycles.
- **Systemic Integration:** Effective no-till relies on a combination of diverse crop rotations, herbicide-based weed control, and harvest weed seed control (HWSC) to replace mechanical cultivation.

## Local Examples
- **Tamman (2006):** A no-till farm achieved a yield of 2 t/ha of wheat during a drought year with only 129 mm of rainfall, demonstrating a water-use efficiency of 17 kg/ha per mm of infiltrated rainfall.
- **Dalwallinu:** Serves as a historical contrast, where pre-1990s tillage led to severe erosion, while post-1990s no-till practices have improved yields even in dry seasons.
- **Mingenew:** Example of tactical moldboard ploughing used to bury resistant weed populations deep in the soil profile.
- **Binnu:** Application of aggressive tillage to manage non-wetting soils that otherwise prevent water infiltration.
