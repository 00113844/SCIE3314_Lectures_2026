# Pulse Width Modulation for Precision Spraying in WA

## Overview
Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) is a precision rate-control technology that regulates chemical application by rapidly switching nozzles on and off via solenoids, rather than altering system pressure. In the Western Australian broadacre context, PWM is critical for decoupling application rate from droplet size, allowing growers to maintain spray quality and minimize drift across varying ground speeds.

## Key Themes
- **Mechanism of Rate Control**: Unlike traditional pressure-based systems—where increasing speed requires a proportional increase in pressure (and thus a shift in droplet spectrum)—PWM utilizes a "duty cycle." By operating at frequencies between 10–100 Hz, the system controls the proportion of time a nozzle remains open to achieve the target rate while maintaining constant pressure.
- **Droplet Size Consistency and Drift Mitigation**: Because pressure remains constant, the droplet size remains stable regardless of the application rate. This is essential for managing highly volatile chemicals, such as 2,4-D, where coarse droplet spectra are required to prevent off-site movement into sensitive areas like vineyards.
- **Operational Flexibility and Turn Compensation**: PWM enables significant speed flexibility (e.g., ranging from 6 km/h to 23 km/h) without compromising spray quality. Furthermore, individual nozzle control allows for automatic duty cycle increases on the outer edges of the boom during turns to compensate for higher ground speeds and prevent under-application.
- **Hardware Compatibility and Limitations**: While PWM offers precision, it introduces specific technical challenges. Air Induction (AI) nozzles may experience "skipping" due to pressure fluctuations caused by the pulsing action; consequently, pre-orifice low-drift nozzles are often preferred. Additionally, low duty cycles combined with narrow fan angles can lead to "striping" (uneven coverage), which is mitigated by increasing boom height or using wider angle nozzles (e.g., 110°).
- **Integration with Regulatory Compliance**: PWM technology supports the rigorous legal requirements for spraying in WA. This includes the ability to precisely execute mixing plans and adhere to weather-monitoring mandates (measuring wind at 2m and temperature/humidity at 1.25m) to ensure environmental safety.

## Regional Patterns
Across WA cropping systems, there is a strong emphasis on integrating PWM with strict 2,4-D volatility regulations, reflecting a regional priority to protect high-value sensitive crops from drift. There is a consistent technical recommendation to operate at 60–80% of maximum speed to maintain an operational buffer for the duty cycle.
