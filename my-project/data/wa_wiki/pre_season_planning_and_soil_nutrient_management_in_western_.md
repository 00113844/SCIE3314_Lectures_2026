# Pre-Season Planning and Soil Nutrient Management in Western Australia

## Summary
Pre-season preparation in Western Australian cropping systems relies on the integration of historical record-keeping, soil capability assessments, and predictive modeling to optimize crop selection and input application. Precision agriculture practices, including systematic soil sampling and DNA-based disease analysis, are employed to mitigate environmental constraints and manage herbicide resistance risks.

## Key Facts
- **Yield Estimation:** Wheat potential is estimated using the French and Schultz equation: $\text{Yield} = (\text{Growing Season Rainfall [May–Oct]} - 110\text{ mm}) \times 20\text{ kg/mm}$.
- **Soil Sampling Methodology:** Nutrient and disease assessments are conducted using Z or W sampling patterns; in no-till systems, cores are primarily taken between nutrient bands to avoid overestimating available fertility.
- **Phosphorus Assessment:** Phosphorus availability is measured via the Colwell bicarbonate test, with the Phosphorus Buffering Index (PBI) used to determine the additional P required to increase soil test levels.
- **Chemical Constraints:** Exchangeable aluminum becomes exponentially toxic in soils with a pH below 4.7 (calcium chloride scale), necessitating the use of lime to reach a target pH of 5.5.
- **Water Holding Capacity:** Soil texture dictates water availability; loams typically offer the highest available water, while sandy soils provide quicker water availability during dry seasons but are prone to nutrient leaching in wet seasons.
- **Resistance Management:** Herbicide resistance risk is stratified by group, with Group A (ACCase inhibitors) and Group B (ALS inhibitors) classified as high-risk.

## Local Examples
- **Cunderdin District:** At decile 5 (median) growing season rainfall of 207 mm, the target wheat yield is calculated at 1.4–1.5 t/ha (representing 75% of potential).
- **Predicta B Analysis:** A specialized DNA-based soil and stubble test used in WA to quantify the presence of crown rot, rhizoctonia, root rot, pythium, and nematodes.
- **Crop-Soil Matching:** Narrow-leaf lupins are utilized in neutral to acidic, non-clay soils, whereas Albus lupins are selected for clay soils with higher pH levels.
