# No-Till Seeding Systems and Management in Western Australia

## Summary
No-till farming in Western Australia is a conservation-based system characterized by minimal soil disturbance and maximum stubble retention to protect soil structure and preserve moisture. The integration of precision guidance and variable rate technology allows for the optimization of seed and fertilizer placement, aligning input application with field-specific yield potential.

## Key Facts
- **Seeding Mechanisms**: WA systems primarily utilize either tine (knife point) or disc seeding. Tine seeders create a narrow groove and are preferred in compact soils, while disc seeders use coulters to cut slots and are more efficient in high-residue or stony environments.
- **Herbicide Interaction**: Tine seeders enhance crop safety by throwing herbicide-treated soil into the inter-rows; conversely, disc seeders may leave herbicides in the crop row, increasing the risk of reduced crop vigor.
- **Resource Efficiency**: Disc seeders are more fuel-efficient, consuming approximately 2 L/ha compared to 4–5 L/ha for tine seeders and 30 L/ha for traditional mouldboard ploughs.
- **Precision Application**: Modern seeders employ precision guidance to implement variable rate farming, targeting high-yield zones with increased inputs to maximize profitability.
- **Soil-Seed Contact**: Press wheels are critical for firming the soil over the seed, which maximizes moisture contact and facilitates water harvesting.
- **Cultural Controls**: Because tillage is minimized, the system relies heavily on harvest weed seed control, disease-resistant cultivars, and diverse crop rotations to maintain soil health.

## Local Examples
- **DBS Seeder**: A prominent example of a knife point (tine) seeding system used in WA.
- **Tungsten-Tipped Tines**: Used for durability in abrasive WA soils, capable of covering 10,000 to 20,000 hectares per season.
- **Drill Seeding**: The standard planting pattern used across Western Australian dryland systems to ensure uniform row placement.
