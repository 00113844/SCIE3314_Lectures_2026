# No-Till Systems and Soil Conservation in Western Australia

## Summary
No-till farming in Western Australia is a holistic management system designed to protect fragile, highly weathered soils by minimizing mechanical disturbance and maximizing stubble retention. By integrating biological principles with precision technologies—specifically Controlled Traffic Farming (CTF) and GPS guidance—WA growers optimize water use efficiency and soil organic carbon levels to maximize grain yield in moisture-limited environments.

## Key Facts
- **Soil Fragility:** Western Australian soils are among the most weathered and nutrient-poor globally, requiring a shift from "dominating" the land to working within biological constraints to prevent degradation.
- **No-Till Mechanics:** Systems typically disturb only 5–20% of the soil surface. Tine seeders are predominantly used in WA due to their superior ability to incorporate pre-emergent herbicides and adapt to harder soil profiles compared to disc seeders.
- **Water Use Efficiency (WUE):** Stubble retention reduces soil evaporation, lowers soil temperatures, and improves water infiltration, allowing for earlier crop establishment and better utilization of summer rainfall.
- **Controlled Traffic Farming (CTF):** To prevent the re-compaction of soil in no-till systems, GPS-guided CTF (accurate to within 2 cm) is used to confine all machinery traffic to permanent lanes, protecting the crop zone.
- **Carbon Sequestration:** Minimizing tillage reduces the introduction of oxygen into the soil profile, thereby limiting the oxidation of organic matter and the subsequent loss of carbon as $\text{CO}_2$.
- **Strategic Tillage:** While generally avoided, tillage (e.g., mouldboard ploughing or deep ripping) is employed strategically to manage non-wetting soils caused by waxy residues or to bury herbicide-resistant weed seeds.

## Local Examples
- **Merriman Region:** Cited as an example of rotation challenges, where 83% of fields are under continuous wheat, limiting the efficacy of no-till disease and weed management.
- **WA Grain Belt Distribution:** Approximately 65% of the area is dedicated to wheat, with canola accounting for 16%.
- **CTF Performance:** In dry years (e.g., 129 mm growing season rainfall), CTF systems have demonstrated high efficiency, achieving yields of 70 kg of grain per mm of infiltrated rainfall.
