# Integrated Weed Management and Seed Bank Dynamics in Western Australia

## Summary
Integrated Weed Management (IWM) in Western Australia focuses on the strategic reduction of weed seed banks to combat increasing herbicide resistance in dryland cropping systems. By combining biological insights into seed decay and dormancy with precision application technologies and harvest-time interventions, growers can maintain yield potential and soil moisture.

## Key Facts
- **Primary Weed Threats:** Annual ryegrass (*Lolium rigidum*) and wild radish are the most significant weeds in WA; both are outcrossing species with high genetic variability, which accelerates the spread of herbicide resistance.
- **Seed Bank Dynamics:** Ryegrass seeds are fragile and decay rapidly on the soil surface; consequently, two to three years of effective control can nearly eliminate the seed bank. In contrast, wild radish exhibits higher dormancy and staggered germination, leading to greater persistence.
- **Tillage Impacts:** No-till systems accelerate the biological decay of ryegrass seeds compared to tillage systems, where buried seeds are protected from surface conditions and can persist for up to five years.
- **Herbicide Strategies:** Management involves a sequence of "knockdown" herbicides (e.g., glyphosate, paraquat) for pre-seeding clearance, followed by rotating pre-emergent herbicides with diverse modes of action to prevent resistance.
- **Harvest Weed Seed Control (HWSC):** Because ryegrass and wild radish are non-shattering, they are susceptible to HWSC methods such as narrow windrow burning, chaff carts, and mechanical seed destructors.
- **Biological Competition:** Crop competitiveness varies by species, with barley generally being more competitive than wheat, canola, or legumes in suppressing weed growth.

## Local Examples
- **WeedSeeker:** A precision agriculture technology using light reflection to detect chlorophyll, reducing herbicide application rates by up to 90%.
- **Seed Destructors:** The use of the Harrington Seed Destructor and Seed Terminator to destroy up to 95% of weed seeds during the harvest process.
- **RIM (Ryegrass Integrated Management):** A modeling tool used to simulate long-term population dynamics and the impact of various management strategies on ryegrass.
- **Northampton CSIRO Trials:** Research demonstrating that while seed predation by ants and termites occurs in no-till systems, it is insufficient to replace chemical control.
