# Spray Nozzle Technology and Application Systems in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the technical distinctions between Controlled Droplet Application (CDA) and hydraulic nozzle systems used in Western Australian agriculture. It emphasizes the critical relationship between nozzle selection, droplet uniformity, and boom geometry to optimize chemical efficacy while minimizing off-target drift in broadacre systems.

## Key Facts
- **Hydraulic Nozzles:** The primary standard for WA broadacre cropping; they operate via pressure to force liquid through an orifice, producing a spectrum of droplet sizes.
- **Controlled Droplet Application (CDA):** Utilizes centrifugal force via spinning discs to create highly uniform droplets, allowing for ultra-low volume (ULV) applications of less than 5 L/ha.
- **Flat Fan Nozzles:** The most prevalent hydraulic type in WA, specifically the 110-degree variant, which allows for wider coverage and fewer nozzles per boom.
- **Drift Mitigation:** Air Induction (AI) nozzles reduce drift by incorporating air bubbles into the droplet; these larger droplets shatter upon leaf impact to ensure adequate coverage.
- **Boom Geometry:** Precise height is essential for hydraulic overlap; for 110-degree nozzles with 50 cm spacing, the optimal height above the target is 35 cm.
- **Aerial Application:** CDA technology is employed via aircraft for rapid response to crop diseases or when boggy soil conditions render ground-based tractors impractical.

## Local Examples
- **Broadacre Standard:** Widespread use of 110-degree flat fan nozzles across Western Australian grain belts for efficient coverage.
- **Aerial Inputs:** Use of aircraft-mounted CDA systems for the application of liquid fertilizers, such as Urea Ammonium Nitrate (UAN).

## Relevant Crops
General agronomy (including broadacre cereals, vineyards, and tree crops)
