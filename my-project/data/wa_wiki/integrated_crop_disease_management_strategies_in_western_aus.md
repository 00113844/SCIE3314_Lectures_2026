# Integrated Crop Disease Management Strategies in Western Australia

## Summary
Crop disease management in Western Australian (WA) dryland systems relies on an integrated approach combining chemical control, cultural practices, and host resistance. Given the low-profit margins of annual cropping, the strategic integration of these methods is essential to ensure that disease control measures remain economically viable while mitigating the risk of pathogen resistance.

## Key Facts
- **Fungicide Application Efficiency**: Application methods vary significantly in their environmental footprint; seed dressings are the most targeted (contacting ~58 $\text{m}^2$), whereas foliar sprays have the broadest impact (contacting ~10,000 $\text{m}^2$).
- **Chemical Control Mechanisms**: Fungicides are classified as protectants (acting on the plant surface to prevent penetration) or systemics (absorbed into the plant to inhibit colonization and sporulation).
- **Economic Thresholds**: For pea black spot, profitability is only maintained when disease severity is kept at or below 2 (on a 0–5 scale). In-furrow fungicides can enable earlier sowing (mid-June) compared to delayed sowing (early July) to achieve this threshold.
- **Host Resistance Dynamics**: Single-gene resistance offers high immunity but is susceptible to rapid collapse (e.g., the 2003 Eyre Peninsula failure). Polygenic resistance provides more durable, long-term protection through structural barriers like lignin deposition.
- **Predictive Modeling**: Blackleg spore showers are forecasted using a specific environmental trigger: a 10-day temperature average of $\le 22^\circ\text{C}$ and 7-day rainfall of $> 2\text{ mm}$.

## Local Examples
- **Pea Black Spot Manager**: A WA Department of Agriculture tool that utilizes historical production data, wind direction, and sowing times to map disease risk across 17 km x 17 km areas.
- **Walden Hills Trials**: Field trials demonstrating that in-furrow fungicide application (e.g., Impact) adds $20–25/ha$ in value for canola varieties with intermediate resistance.
- **Regional Spore Variation**: In southern WA (e.g., Mount Barker), blackleg spores release as early as mid-March, whereas in northern WA, release typically begins in late July, allowing for a wider hazard-free sowing window.
