# No-Till Farming Systems in Western Australia

## Summary
No-till farming in Western Australia is a strategic agronomic system designed to maximize water use efficiency by converting limited soil moisture into grain yield. It integrates precision technology, such as RTK auto-steer, with biological soil management to maintain soil integrity and productivity in arid environments.

## Key Facts
- **Core Components**: The system relies on five pillars: reduced soil disturbance, residue retention, diverse crop rotations, cultural weed control, and controlled traffic.
- **Moisture Conservation**: Stubble retention acts as an insulating layer that reduces evaporation, prevents soil sealing from raindrop impact, and increases water infiltration.
- **Controlled Traffic Farming (CTF)**: Utilizes GPS and RTK auto-steer (accurate to 2 cm) to confine all wheel traffic to permanent tramlines, eliminating compaction in the crop growing zone.
- **Strategic Tillage**: While generally avoided, tillage is used remedially to address non-wetting sandy soils, incorporate lime into acidic subsoils, or manage resistant weed seeds.
- **Soil Management Tools**: Specialized machinery such as delvers are employed in duplex soils to scrape clay from depths exceeding one meter to improve the wettability of sandy surface soils.
- **Agronomic Risks**: Improper tillage can lead to moisture loss, increased soil erosion, the release of soil carbon as $\text{CO}_2$, and the surfacing of toxic subsoils (e.g., high boron).

## Local Examples
- **Colin Hutchinson (Urukoin)**: Demonstrated superior water use efficiency of 17 kg/mm/ha (compared to a neighbor's 9 kg/mm/ha) through strict adherence to no-till and CTF.
- **Rod Messina (Minunu)**: Utilized mouldboard ploughing as a strategic remedial tactic to bury non-wetting soil and resistant weed seeds.
- **Una Farm Tour**: Provided a field demonstration of non-wetting soils where water pooled for over 2.5 hours without infiltrating the soil profile.
- **Yield Impact**: Observed yield increases from 1.7 to 2.5 tonnes per hectare following mouldboard ploughing on non-wetting sands.
