# Crop Disease Management and Pathogen Dynamics in Western Australia

## Summary
This page examines the primary fungal, viral, and nematode pathogens affecting Western Australian cropping systems, detailing their impact on yield, grain quality, and overall farm profitability. Management focuses on the integration of resistant cultivars, strategic crop rotations, and precision chemical applications to mitigate the risks posed by evolving pathogen strains and increasing fungicide resistance.

## Key Facts
- **Loss Categories**: Diseases cause direct losses (reduced seed yield, size, viability, and nutritional value) and indirect losses (wasted fertilizer inputs and the financial cost of control measures).
- **Cereal Rust Dynamics**: Stem, leaf, and stripe rusts are managed by removing cereal volunteers to eliminate inoculum carryover and utilizing resistant varieties; in WA, these strains typically spread from west to east via prevailing winds.
- **Environmental Influence**: Changing climate patterns in WA, specifically reduced rainfall, have shifted disease prevalence, increasing the incidence of Fusarium crown rot while decreasing Septoria nodorum blotch.
- **Fungicide Resistance**: Pathogens such as powdery mildew and barley net blotch are developing resistance to key chemical groups, including strobilurins, DMI, and SDHI, necessitating integrated pest management (IPM).
- **Soil-Borne Pathogens**: Take-all is re-emerging in high-rainfall regions due to continuous wheat cropping, while Rhizoctonia and root lesion nematodes cause significant systemic losses across southern Australia.
- **Pulse-Specific Management**: Lupin brown spot is managed through deeper sowing (5 cm) to avoid high spore concentrations near the soil surface, while pea black spot and chickpea ascochyta are managed via delayed sowing and stubble management.

## Local Examples
- **Fusarium Crown Rot**: Causes approximately $100 million in annual national losses, with high prevalence in the drier eastern wheat belts of WA.
- **Canola Blackleg**: Remains a primary constraint in WA brassica production, resulting in annual losses between $20–50 million.
- **UG99 Stem Rust**: An exotic strain that poses a high biosecurity risk to WA via wind currents crossing the Indian Ocean from South Africa.
- **Yid Pea Wheat**: A specific resistant variety used to manage and reduce future infestations of cereal cyst nematode.
