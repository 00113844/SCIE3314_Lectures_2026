# Grain Protein Management for Malt Barley in Western Australia

## Summary
Malt barley production in Western Australia requires precise management to maintain grain protein within a narrow window of 9.5% to 12.5% to meet brewing specifications. Precision agriculture strategies focus on balancing nitrogen (N) supply—derived from soil organic carbon, legume rotations, and fertilizer—against the "dilution effect" of yield to optimize grain quality and grade.

## Key Facts
- **Protein Window:** Protein levels above 12.5% impair brewery filtration and beer clarity (haze), while levels below 9.5% reduce beer head duration and yeast fermentation efficiency.
- **Nitrogen Drivers:** Protein content is primarily determined by soil nitrogen availability, which is influenced by rainfall zones, soil organic carbon thresholds (e.g., 0.6% in medium rainfall zones), and previous crop rotations.
- **The Dilution Effect:** Higher grain yields tend to lower protein concentration because a fixed amount of available nitrogen is distributed across a larger volume of grain.
- **Fertilizer Impact:** The rate of nitrogen application has a more significant influence on final grain protein than the timing of application.
- **Timing and Quality:** While nitrogen timing has minimal impact on total yield, applying N at stem elongation (Z31) can increase the proportion of grain achieving the premium Malt 1 grade.
- **Management Risk:** Following a legume rotation increases the risk of exceeding the protein window, particularly in dry seasons where low yields fail to dilute the high background nitrogen.

## Local Examples
- **Varieties:** Bass and Sterling are noted for having higher protein tendencies compared to other WA varieties.
- **Decision Support Tools:** SYN (Select Your Nitrogen), Pi Cal, and Yield Profit are used to assist in nitrogen management and protein modeling.
- **Soil Metrics:** Nitrogen availability estimates (e.g., ~110 kg/ha) are based on specific WA conditions, such as low rainfall areas with organic carbon >0.6% following a legume.
