# Evaporative Demand and Spray Drift Management

## Overview
In Western Australian broadacre cropping, managing the interaction between atmospheric conditions and droplet physics is critical to ensuring chemical efficacy and preventing off-target movement. The primary metric for this management is Delta T—the difference between dry bulb and wet bulb temperatures—which serves as a proxy for the evaporative power of the air.

## Key Themes
- **Delta T and Droplet Stability**: Delta T is used to quantify the risk of droplet evaporation; a threshold of $< 8^\circ\text{C}$ is recommended to prevent droplets from shrinking rapidly, which would otherwise increase their susceptibility to wind drift.
- **Volume Median Diameter (VMD) Control**: Nozzle selection directly dictates VMD; Air Induction (AI) nozzles produce larger droplets ($500\text{--}600\mu\text{m}$) that significantly reduce drift to $\sim 1\%$, whereas flat fan nozzles produce smaller droplets ($\sim 200\mu\text{m}$) with drift rates as high as $18\%$.
- **Pressure-Droplet Correlation**: There is an inverse relationship between spray pressure and droplet size; increasing pressure reduces VMD, thereby enhancing coverage for specific targets (e.g., budworm) but elevating the risk of drift.
- **Atmospheric Stability and Inversions**: While wind is necessary to prevent temperature inversions—where cold air traps droplets and causes unpredictable long-distance movement—optimal wind speeds must be maintained between $3\text{--}15\text{ km/h}$.
- **Calibration Precision in Variable Terrain**: Accurate application rates (L/ha) require in-field speed verification over set distances to account for wheel slip on soft WA soils, as dashboard readings often overestimate actual ground speed.
- **Logistical Volume Constraints**: Broadacre application in WA typically ranges from $60\text{--}90\text{ L/ha}$ to balance the need for adequate coverage with the logistical challenges of water transport across vast paddock scales.

## Regional Patterns
WA growers rely heavily on Bureau of Meteorology (BoM) data to synchronize spraying windows with low Delta T and moderate wind speeds, reflecting a systemic shift away from shielded sprayers toward high-precision AI nozzle technology.
