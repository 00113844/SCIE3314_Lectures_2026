# Weed Biology and Seed Bank Management in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the biological characteristics of Annual Ryegrass (*Lolium rigidum*) and Wild Radish, the two most significant weeds in Western Australian cropping systems. Understanding their seed bank dynamics, germination patterns, and non-shattering seed traits is essential for implementing precision integrated weed management and Harvest Weed Seed Control (HWSC) strategies.

## Key Facts
- **Annual Ryegrass Dynamics:** A winter annual grass with high fecundity (up to 45,000 seeds/m²) and a short seed bank life (1–2 years), meaning consistent prevention of seed set over 2–3 years can effectively deplete populations.
- **Wild Radish Persistence:** A broadleaf weed with higher dormancy and staggered germination than ryegrass, driven by durable pods and seed coats that protect the embryo from environmental degradation.
- **Seed Bank Inputs and Outputs:** The seed bank is managed by controlling "seed rain" (inputs) and maximizing outputs through germination, biological decay, and predation.
- **No-Till Influence:** No-till systems generally accelerate ryegrass seed decay due to increased moisture and microbial activity, although excessive stubble can potentially impede seed predation by ants.
- **Non-Shattering Traits:** Both ryegrass and wild radish seeds remain on the plant at maturity, a biological vulnerability that allows for the physical capture and destruction of seeds during harvest.
- **Herbicide Resistance:** Both species are outcrossing and genetically variable, leading to rapid spread of resistance; resistance in wild radish is notably higher in northern WA than in southern coastal regions.

## Local Examples
- **Yield Impact:** Wild radish can cause yield losses of up to 50% in lupins and up to 91% in canola at high densities (64 plants/m²).
- **Regional Variation:** Southern coastal areas of WA exhibit lower wild radish resistance due to higher rainfall, increased livestock grazing, and reduced reliance on chemical controls compared to northern districts.
