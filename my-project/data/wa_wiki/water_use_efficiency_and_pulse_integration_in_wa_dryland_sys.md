# Water Use Efficiency and Pulse Integration in WA Dryland Systems

## Summary
Water Use Efficiency (WUE) serves as a critical diagnostic metric for evaluating crop productivity in Western Australia's Mediterranean dryland systems, where rainfall is the primary limiting factor. The integration of pulses into cropping rotations enhances overall system WUE by improving soil nitrogen levels and reducing the incidence of cereal diseases.

## Key Facts
- **WUE Definition:** Calculated as the yield per unit area divided by the water used per unit area, providing a benchmark for spatial and temporal performance.
- **Wheat Productivity Benchmarks:** The theoretical upper limit for wheat WUE in Australia is approximately 20 kg/ha/mm, though some high-performing cases reach 25 kg/ha/mm.
- **Evaporative Loss:** Soil evaporation is a major constraint to yield potential, with estimated losses of approximately 110 mm in certain studies.
- **Rotational Benefits:** Integrating pulses significantly improves wheat health; for example, wheat following lupins can see a reduction in take-all disease prevalence from 54% to 15%.
- **Water Footprint:** Pulses are highly efficient protein sources, requiring significantly less water (1,250 L/kg) compared to beef (30,000 L/kg).
- **Soil-Crop Compatibility:** Lupins are well-adapted to the sandy, acidic soils of WA, whereas chickpeas are generally limited by acidic or heavy soil types.

## Local Examples
- **Morawa (Northern Wheatbelt):** Actual WUE has been recorded as low as 4.93 kg/ha/mm, highlighting a significant yield gap compared to the 20 kg/ha/mm potential.
- **Kimberley Large Chickpea:** A specific variety developed for disease resistance to increase farmer revenue.
- **WA Production Trends:** Wheat production has scaled from 6–7 million tonnes in the 1980s to 24 million tonnes in recent years due to improved agronomic practices.
