# Grain Screenings and Plumpness Management in WA

## Overview
Grain screenings refer to the percentage of a crop that passes through a standardized slotted sieve (typically 2.0 mm to 2.2 mm), serving as a primary metric for grain plumpness. In Western Australian precision agriculture, managing screenings is critical because plumpness determines water absorption rates and overall grain weight, directly impacting the malting potential of barley and the market grade of wheat.

## Key Themes
- **Environmental Determinants of Plumpness:** A "soft finish"—defined by ample moisture availability during the grain-filling stage—is the most significant driver for reducing screenings and increasing yield.
- **Abiotic Stress Impacts:** Frost damage during the milky to soft dough stages can cause catastrophic increases in screenings (up to 70%), a risk that is often exacerbated by high stubble levels which reduce soil heat radiation.
- **Biotic Stress and Disease:** Pathogens, particularly powdery mildew, net blotch, and scald, significantly impair grain filling. The use of fungicides and the selection of varieties with Moderate Resistance to Moderate Susceptibility (MR-MS) are essential for maintaining low screenings.
- **Nutrient-Moisture Interaction:** While nitrogen is required for yield, excessive nitrogen application during dry spring conditions can paradoxically increase screenings, highlighting the need for precision nitrogen management.
- **Agronomic Influences:** Weed competition, specifically from ryegrass, is a known driver of increased screenings, whereas factors such as seed rate and row spacing have been found to have negligible effects on grain plumpness.
- **Industry Quality Thresholds:** Strict market specifications exist to avoid quality penalties, with target screenings generally below 5% for wheat and between 7% and 10% for malting barley.

## Regional Patterns
Across WA cropping systems, there is a consistent correlation between moisture stress during the final growth stages and high screenings, though varietal performance varies significantly (e.g., Vlaming and Lambing generally outperform Bullock or Flagship in plumpness).
