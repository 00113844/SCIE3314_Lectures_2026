# Agricultural Pest Ecology and Management in Western Australia

## Summary
Agricultural pests are defined anthropocentrically based on their negative impact on human interests, with their status varying by geographic location and production system. In Western Australia, managing these pests requires an understanding of pest ecology—specifically the distinction between generalist and specialist feeders—and the implementation of Integrated Pest Management (IPM) and strict biosecurity protocols.

## Key Facts
- **Pest Status and Context**: A species is classified as a pest based on its impact on human economic or structural interests; therefore, pest status is context-dependent and varies across different production systems.
- **Pest Release Phenomenon**: Crops grown outside their center of origin often experience an initial productivity boost due to "release" from native pests and diseases, though this often leads to a lack of bred-in resistance and eventual production collapse upon pest invasion.
- **Generalists vs. Specialists**: Generalist pests (e.g., rabbits, locusts) possess broad diets and rapid breeding capabilities, while specialist pests (e.g., Russian wheat aphid) are often monophagous and can cause severe damage by vectoring viruses.
- **Economic Impact**: Damage is measured not only by yield loss (volume) but also by quality degradation. For example, snail contamination in harvests can reduce sale prices even if the total grain volume remains constant.
- **Biosecurity**: Australia's historical isolation has prevented the establishment of devastating pests such as screwworm flies and citrus canker, highlighting the critical role of biosecurity in maintaining agricultural productivity.

## Local Examples
- **Red-legged Earth Mites**: Introduced from South Africa, these mites damage seedlings by sucking cell contents, severely impacting early plant development.
- **Russian Wheat Aphid**: A specialist pest in Australia that poses a significant threat primarily through the transmission of viruses.
- **Mouse Plagues (*Mus domesticus*)**: Periodic outbreaks that cause dramatic damage to grain crops, particularly noted in South Australia.
- **Slaters (Isopods)**: Occasional pests in wheat and canola systems, particularly in environments where stubble is retained, as they prefer decaying organic matter but may attack seedlings.
