# Global Grain Production and Sustainable Intensification in WA

## Summary
This topic examines the critical need for sustainable intensification in agriculture as global food demand rises against a static backdrop of available cropland. For Western Australian producers, this necessitates leveraging biotechnology and conservation tillage to maintain export competitiveness in wheat and canola while maximizing the potential of locally adapted species.

## Key Facts
- **Sustainable Intensification:** With global cropland remaining static, increasing yields on existing land is the primary mechanism to meet the food requirements of a population projected to reach 8–10 billion by mid-century.
- **Yield Variability:** Wheat yields in rain-fed systems average approximately 3 t/ha globally, but a wide variance (up to 17 t/ha) indicates a significant yield gap that can be addressed through precision agronomy.
- **Biotechnology and Soil Health:** The adoption of genetically modified (GM) crops, such as Roundup Ready varieties, has acted as a catalyst for the expansion of no-till farming by enabling efficient post-emergence weed control.
- **Conservation Agriculture:** No-till systems reduce soil erosion and land preparation time by leaving crop residues on the surface, a model that is critical for long-term soil health in dryland systems.
- **Export Dynamics:** Australia remains a major global exporter of wheat and canola, though it faces increasing competition from Russia and Eastern European producers.

## Local Examples
- **Lupin Production:** Australia dominates the global lupin market due to the successful breeding and domestication of locally adapted species.
- **Export Markets:** Australia is identified as a significant global player in both the wheat and canola sectors.
