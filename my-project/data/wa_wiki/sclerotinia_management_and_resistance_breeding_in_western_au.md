# Sclerotinia Management and Resistance Breeding in Western Australia

## Summary
Sclerotinia is one of the most challenging diseases to manage in Western Australian cropping systems due to its broad host range and the extreme persistence of soil-borne sclerotia. Because chemical and cultural controls are often cost-prohibitive or ineffective in WA's sandy soils and Mediterranean climate, the development of genetically resistant cultivars is the only sustainable long-term strategy for mitigating yield losses.

## Key Facts
- **Impact and Host Range:** Sclerotinia affects all pulse crops and non-cereal plants, with canola and rapeseed experiencing yield losses of up to 45% in heavily affected fields.
- **Pathogen Persistence:** Sclerotia (resting structures) can survive in Western Australia's sandy soils for up to 10 years, rendering standard crop rotations ineffective unless cereals are grown exclusively for extended periods.
- **Chemical Control Limitations:** Fungicide applications (e.g., Prezara) are costly (approx. $80/ha for two sprays) and often inefficient due to the difficulty of timing applications with variable infection windows.
- **WA Pathotype Distribution:** Research identifies three primary Brassica pathotypes in WA, with Pathotype 76 being the most dominant, comprising 74% of the pathogen population.
- **Resistance Mechanisms:** Effective resistance is characterized by physical barriers, such as increased cortical layer thickness in stems and lignin deposition, which prevent the pathogen from penetrating vascular tissues.
- **Screening Methodology:** To eliminate variability caused by differing flowering times, researchers use stem inoculation with agar plugs to measure lesion length, which correlates strongly with field survival.

## Local Examples
- **Shenton Park Field Station:** Used as a primary site for screening thousands of Brassica lines against prevailing WA pathotypes.
- **Pathotype 76:** The dominant strain of Sclerotinia in Western Australia.
- **Variety 'Mystic':** A historical example of a highly resistant canola variety (pre-2000) that represents a genetic resource for modern breeding.
