# Precision Soil Acidity and Liming Management

## Overview
Soil acidity is a critical environmental constraint in Western Australian (WA) cropping systems, primarily manifesting as nutrient deficiencies and aluminum toxicity. Precision management focuses on maintaining soil pH above critical thresholds to optimize root elongation and nutrient uptake, utilizing decision-support tools to balance the high cost of lime application against long-term yield gains.

## Key Themes
- **Critical pH Thresholds and Toxicity**: Agronomic productivity is severely limited when surface soils drop below pH 5.5 (limiting nutrient availability) and subsoils drop below pH 4.8. The latter is particularly detrimental as it triggers the release of toxic aluminum, which inhibits root elongation and reduces the plant's capacity for moisture uptake.
- **Economic Implications**: Soil acidification represents a significant systemic risk to the WA agricultural economy, with estimated annual yield losses ranging between $500 million and $1,500 million.
- **Precision Decision Support (iLime)**: The transition from blanket application to precision liming is driven by tools like the iLime app (powered by the Oplime engine). This tool allows growers to simulate pH trajectories and profitability over 20-year horizons with high predictive accuracy (R-squared of 0.8).
- **Biogeochemical Drivers of Acidification**: Acidity is accelerated by the continuous export of basic cations through harvested grains and the leaching of nitrates, which releases protons into the soil profile.
- **Agronomic Trade-offs**: While liming corrects pH, it can introduce secondary constraints, including induced manganese (Mn) deficiency—requiring foliar correction—and an increased prevalence of take-all disease and snail populations.
- **Advanced Soil Re-engineering**: To address combined acidity and compaction, experimental "deep soil re-engineering" involves incorporating lime and organic matter to depths of 70–90 cm, which has demonstrated the potential to double yields in specific contexts.

## Regional Patterns
Management trends show significant improvement in the Avon River Basin, where the proportion of topsoil below pH 5.5 has decreased from 72% to 46%. However, high soil variability in the Eastern Wheatbelt continues to necessitate rigorous ground-truthing of digital lime recommendations to ensure accuracy.
