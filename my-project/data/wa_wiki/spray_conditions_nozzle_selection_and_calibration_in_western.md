# Spray Conditions, Nozzle Selection, and Calibration in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the technical relationship between nozzle selection, environmental conditions, and sprayer calibration to optimize chemical efficacy and minimize off-target drift. In the Western Australian broadacre context, managing the Volume Median Diameter (VMD) of droplets and monitoring evaporative demand (Delta T) are critical for ensuring precise application and environmental safety.

## Key Facts
- **Nozzle Technology and Drift**: Nozzle selection significantly impacts drift potential. Flat fan nozzles produce the smallest droplets ($\sim 200\mu\text{m}$) with high drift ($\sim 16\text{--}17\%$), whereas Air Induction (AI) nozzles produce much larger droplets ($500\text{--}600\mu\text{m}$) and reduce drift to approximately $1\%$.
- **Pressure Dynamics**: Increasing spray pressure (e.g., from $2.75$ to $5$ bar) reduces droplet size, which subsequently increases the risk of spray drift.
- **Environmental Constraints (Delta T)**: The "Delta T" (difference between dry bulb and wet bulb temperatures) is a primary indicator of evaporative power. For optimal spraying, Delta T should be less than $8^\circ\text{C}$, and ambient temperatures should ideally remain below $28^\circ\text{C}$ to prevent droplets from shrinking and drifting.
- **Wind Speed Requirements**: Ideal wind speeds range between $3\text{--}15\text{ km/h}$. Completely still air is avoided due to the risk of temperature inversions, which can cause spray to move unexpectedly over long distances.
- **WA Application Rates**: Due to large field sizes and water transport logistics, typical broadacre application rates in WA are $60\text{--}90\text{ L/ha}$, though soil-applied herbicides may require $150\text{--}400\text{ L/ha}$ for improved coverage.
- **Calibration Precision**: Accurate calibration requires measuring travel speed over a set distance (e.g., $100\text{m}$) in-field to account for wheel slip on soft soils, rather than relying on dashboard readings.

## Local Examples
- **Broadacre Water Volume**: Standard WA application rates of $60\text{--}90\text{ L/ha}$ are utilized to balance coverage with the logistical challenges of water transport across large paddocks.
- **Chemical Application**: The use of Sakura herbicide (pyroxysulfone) at a rate of $118\text{ g/ha}$ serves as a model for calculating chemical dosing per tank based on sprayer output.
