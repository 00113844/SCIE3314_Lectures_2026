# Cool Season Grain Legumes in Western Australian Dryland Systems

## Summary
This topic examines the integration of cool-season pulses into Western Australia's Mediterranean dryland cropping systems to enhance soil fertility and system resilience. From a precision agriculture perspective, optimizing pulse production requires a deep understanding of species-environment interactions, specifically the relationship between soil pH, clay content, and rainfall distribution.

## Key Facts
- **The Legume Effect:** Integrating pulses into rotations significantly increases subsequent wheat yields (86–203% increase) compared to continuous wheat cropping.
- **Nitrogen Fixation:** Pulses provide substantial biological nitrogen fixation; for example, faba beans can fix up to 192 kg/ha, leaving residual nitrogen (7–70 kg/ha) for the following crop.
- **Disease Suppression:** Pulse rotations reduce the incidence of root diseases; wheat following lupins shows only 15% take-all infection compared to 54% in wheat-after-wheat systems.
- **Environmental Sustainability:** Pulse integration reduces the overall carbon footprint of cropping systems by 20–35% and increases soil organic carbon.
- **Species-Environment Interaction:** Crop selection is driven by soil and climate: faba beans thrive in high pH, heavy clay soils in high-rainfall areas, whereas field peas are better suited for low-rainfall and late-sowing conditions.
- **Drought Adaptation:** Field peas, lentils, and chickpeas exhibit osmotic adjustment, allowing them to outperform faba beans and lupins under severe water stress.

## Local Examples
- **Narrowleaf Lupin:** A cornerstone of WA agriculture, contributing 90% of national production and adapted to coarse, acidic to neutral soils.
- **Ord River Region:** Production of high-value large Kabuli chickpeas, which command premium prices ($2,000–2,500/ton).
- **Regional Rainfall Variability:** Contrast in rainfall distribution between northern WA and southern districts such as Katanning.

## Relevant Crops
Narrowleaf lupin, field pea, chickpea, faba bean, lentil
