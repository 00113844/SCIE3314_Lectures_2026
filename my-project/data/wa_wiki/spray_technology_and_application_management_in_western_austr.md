# Spray Technology and Application Management in Western Australia

## Summary
Effective pesticide application in Western Australian broad-acre systems relies on the precise management of droplet size, nozzle selection, and environmental conditions to maximize chemical efficacy and minimize off-target drift. The integration of precision technologies, such as Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) and sectional control, is critical for reducing input waste and improving the uniformity of application across variable landscapes.

## Key Facts
- **Droplet Size Metrics**: Application efficacy is measured by Volume Median Diameter (VMD), where 50% of the spray volume is smaller and 50% is larger than the median diameter; smaller droplets increase coverage but elevate drift risk.
- **Nozzle Technology**: Hydraulic nozzles are standard for broad-acre use, while Controlled Droplet Applicators (CDA) use spinning disks to produce uniform droplets (50–100 microns) for ultra-low volume (ULV) applications.
- **Drift Mitigation**: Air Induction (AI) nozzles reduce drift by incorporating air bubbles into the droplets, increasing their size and reducing the proportion of fine, drift-prone particles.
- **Environmental Constraints**: To minimize evaporation and drift, spraying should ideally occur when the Delta T (difference between wet and dry bulb temperatures) is less than 8°C and temperatures remain below 28°C.
- **Atmospheric Risks**: Temperature inversions—where cold air is trapped beneath a layer of warm air—can cause droplets to remain suspended, significantly increasing the risk of long-distance drift.
- **Precision Control**: Modern systems utilize sectional control to prevent over-application and direct injection to separate chemicals from the main water tank, enhancing operator safety.

## Local Examples
- **Standard Hardware**: Yellow O2 nozzles (0.2 US gallons per minute at 40 psi) are widely utilized in WA for medium-sized droplet applications.
- **Application Rates**: Typical spray volumes for WA broad-acre cropping generally range between 60 and 100 liters per hectare.
- **Industry Standards**: The GRDC nozzle selection guide is the primary reference for calibration and nozzle selection in the region.
