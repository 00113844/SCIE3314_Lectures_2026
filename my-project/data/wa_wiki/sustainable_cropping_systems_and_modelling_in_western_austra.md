# Sustainable Cropping Systems and Modelling in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the transition toward sustainable intensification within the Western Australian wheat belt, focusing on the reduction of chemical inputs and the optimization of resource use. It emphasizes the application of data-driven decision-making through crop and weed simulation modelling to mitigate herbicide resistance and enhance nutrient efficiency.

## Key Facts
- **Herbicide Intensity:** Paddock-level herbicide application in the WA wheat belt averages 5.4 sprays per year, creating significant pressure for the development of herbicide-resistant weed populations.
- **Water Use Efficiency:** Despite poor soil quality, WA farmers achieve some of the highest global yields per millimeter of rainfall, highlighting a high level of dryland farming efficiency.
- **Nutrient Management:** Phosphorus management in inland WA typically follows a replacement strategy, where applications are calculated to replace the specific nutrient volumes removed during grain harvest.
- **Precision Application:** Variable rate technology (VRT) is utilized to apply fertilizers based on yield monitor data, minimizing waste and reducing the environmental footprint of nitrogen and phosphorus.
- **Simulation Tools:** Tools such as APSIM (Agricultural Production Simulator) and RIM (Ryegrass Integrated Management) are used to model crop growth stages and long-term herbicide resistance trends based on soil, weather, and management inputs.

## Local Examples
- **Scepter:** A wheat variety used in APSIM modelling to predict flowering, maturity, and harvest timing.
- **UWA Lupins:** Low-alkaloid varieties developed at the University of Western Australia, bred for disease resistance and non-shattering pods.
- **RIM Tool:** An Excel-based modelling tool used specifically to study the impacts of pre- and post-emergent herbicide applications on ryegrass resistance.
- **Regional Sites:** Practical agronomic assessments conducted at Regan's Ford and Cundadine.
