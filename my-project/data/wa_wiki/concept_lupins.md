# Lupin Agronomy and Management in Western Australia

## Overview
Lupins are essential nitrogen-fixing legumes integrated into Western Australian (WA) dryland cropping systems to enhance soil fertility and provide critical disease breaks for cereal crops. They are specifically valued for their ability to thrive in acidic sandy soils, making them a strategic tool for managing soil constraints and optimizing rotational productivity.

## Key Themes
- **Soil-Specific Species Selection**: Crop selection is driven by soil pH; Narrow-leaf lupins (*Lupinus angustifolius*) are optimized for acidic sandy soils, while Albus lupins are utilized in more alkaline conditions.
- **Biological Nitrogen Fixation (BNF)**: Lupins rely on a symbiotic relationship with Group G rhizobia. To maintain fixation efficiency and prevent genetic drift toward less effective natural strains, re-inoculation is required every 5–7 years using peat-based (cool-stored) or granular bentonite-clay inoculants.
- **Micronutrient Criticality**: While nitrogen fertilizer is avoided to prevent the inhibition of rhizobia, phosphorus is essential. Molybdenum is required for effective N-fixation, and manganese (Mn) is critical to prevent seed splitting and discoloration—a deficiency often exacerbated by liming.
- **Establishment and Density**: Agronomic targets focus on a plant density of approximately 45 plants/m², typically achieved with seeding rates of 80–120 kg/ha. The crop exhibits epigeal germination and is often sown at 25 cm row spacing to align with standard cereal equipment.
- **Integrated Weed Management**: Managing broadleaf weeds is a primary constraint. Strategies include the use of triazine-tolerant cultivars for pre-emergent control (e.g., Simazine, Metribuzin), post-emergent Group A herbicides for grasses, and late-season "crop topping" with glyphosate or paraquat to prevent weed seed set.
- **Rotational Synergy**: A traditional 1:1 wheat-lupin rotation is employed to break disease cycles (such as Pleiochaeta root rot) and increase available soil nitrogen for subsequent cereal crops, thereby improving overall system yield.

## Regional Patterns
There is strong consensus across WA agronomic data regarding the 5–7 year inoculation cycle and the strict correlation between species selection and soil pH. While Narrow-leaf lupins dominate the acid sandplains, there is a noted shift in the status of Sandplain lupins, which are now largely regarded as weeds due to high alkaloid levels and anthracnose susceptibility.
