# Spray Application Technology and Calibration in Western Australia

## Summary
Effective chemical application in Western Australian broadacre systems requires the integration of nozzle physics, environmental monitoring, and precise calibration to maximize efficacy and minimize off-target drift. Precision agriculture tools, such as sectional control and GPS-based speed monitoring, are essential for managing the scale of WA paddocks while maintaining strict environmental stewardship.

## Key Facts
- **Droplet Dynamics:** Spray pressure inversely correlates with droplet size; increasing pressure reduces droplet size, which enhances coverage for some targets (e.g., budworm in canola) but significantly increases the risk of spray drift.
- **Nozzle Performance:** Induction nozzles provide superior drift control, often resulting in 1% or less drift at 2 meters, whereas flat fan nozzles can exhibit drift rates between 16% and 18%.
- **Environmental Constraints:** The Delta T (difference between dry bulb and wet bulb temperatures) is used to measure the evaporative power of the air; a Delta T of less than 8°C is recommended to prevent droplets from evaporating before reaching the target.
- **Atmospheric Inversions:** Temperature inversions, common in valleys during the night or early morning, trap cold air and cause droplets to linger, creating a high risk of unpredictable drift.
- **Calibration Principles:** Application rate (L/ha) is calculated using the formula: $\text{L/ha} = \frac{\text{Total spray output (L/min)} \times 600}{\text{Speed (km/h)} \times \text{Swath width (m)}}$.
- **Precision Systems:** Modern sprayers utilize sectional control to prevent overlap and direct injection systems to reduce operator exposure to concentrated chemicals.

## Local Examples
- **Weather Monitoring:** WA farmers utilize Bureau of Meteorology (BoM) data to monitor Delta T, wind speed, and temperature to determine optimal spraying windows.
- **Chemical Application:** Use of Sakura herbicide at specific rates (e.g., 118 g/ha) as a benchmark for tank mixing calculations.
- **Equipment Evolution:** The decline of shielded sprayers in the WA wheat belt due to the impracticality of slow operational speeds over vast paddock areas.
