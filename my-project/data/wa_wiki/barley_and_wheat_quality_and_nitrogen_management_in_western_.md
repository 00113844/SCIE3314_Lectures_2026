# Barley and Wheat Quality and Nitrogen Management in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the agronomic requirements for producing high-quality malting barley and premium wheat within Western Australian cropping systems. It emphasizes the critical balance between yield and quality, specifically focusing on nitrogen (N) remobilization, protein windows, and the application of precision tools like Yield Prophet and Controlled Traffic Farming (CTF) to optimize grain specifications.

## Key Facts
- **Barley Morphology and Type**: WA exclusively cultivates two-rowed spring barley, where only the central floret is fertile, distinguishing it from the six-rowed varieties common in Europe.
- **Malt Quality Windows**: Malt barley is subject to strict quality parameters, most notably a protein window of 9–12.5%; protein levels outside this range result in the grain being downgraded to feed barley.
- **Grain Plumpness Metrics**: Quality is assessed via test weight (kg/hL) and screenings (measured by a 2mm slotted sieve). Malt 1 grade requires $\le 7\%$ screenings, whereas feed grades allow up to 25%.
- **Nitrogen Dynamics**: Cereal N uptake occurs in two stages: initial accumulation for vegetative growth and subsequent remobilization to the grain during filling. Approximately 80–90% of N is accumulated by flowering.
- **Clearfield Technology**: The use of Clearfield varieties (e.g., Spartacus) allows the application of imidazolinone (IMI) herbicides for weed control, though these can persist in acidic soils and risk damaging subsequent non-tolerant crops.
- **Pre-harvest Stress**: Late-season rains (October–November) can induce grain germination, increasing $\alpha$-amylase activity and lowering the "falling number," which degrades dough and malting quality.

## Local Examples
- **Market Pricing**: In the Kwinana zone, quality premiums are evident, with H1 wheat historically commanding higher prices (e.g., \$405/ton) compared to lower grades (\$385/ton).
- **Variety Succession**: Rapid adoption cycles are common in WA, exemplified by the transition from the Scope variety (peaking in 2016) to newer varieties like Spartacus.
- **Precision Modeling**: The use of Yield Prophet models to account for soil N mineralization based on previous rotations (e.g., lupins) and climatic data to guide fertilizer application.
