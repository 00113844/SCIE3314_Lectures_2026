# Data-Driven Nitrogen Management and Yield Prediction in WA Wheat

## Summary
This topic examines the application of decision-support software, specifically Yield Profit (powered by the APSIM model), to optimize nitrogen (N) application and predict grain yields in Western Australian cropping systems. By integrating real-time soil data, weather patterns, and crop phenology, growers can transition from static fertilizer plans to dynamic, data-driven nutrient management.

## Key Facts
- **Mineral vs. Total Nitrogen**: Agronomic recommendations are based on mineral nitrogen (the sum of nitrate and ammonium), which is plant-available, rather than total nitrogen, which includes unavailable organic forms.
- **Concentration Conversion**: Bulk density measurements are essential for converting soil nitrogen concentrations measured in mg/kg to field-applicable rates in kg/ha.
- **Nitrogen Mineralization**: The APSIM model estimates the additional available nitrogen released throughout the season via the breakdown of organic matter.
- **Water-Limited Yield Potential**: This represents the maximum yield achievable under ideal nutrition; in high-rainfall years (decile 8–9), this potential can reach 4 to 6.5 t/ha in certain WA regions.
- **Economic Optimization**: Nitrogen profit reports allow for scenario testing (e.g., split applications vs. single doses) by incorporating crop sale prices, fertilizer costs, and application overheads.
- **Tillage Impact**: Low-tillage or no-till systems preserve organic carbon, whereas excessive tillage may cause a temporary increase in nitrogen release while depleting long-term soil carbon.

## Local Examples
- **Dandaragan Paddock**: A case study involving wheat variety 'Sceptre' at growth stage 3.1, exhibiting severe nitrogen stress despite low water stress.
- **Yield Profit Software**: A tool utilized by the Birchip Cropping Group to manage paddock-specific data and generate APSIM-based yield predictions.
- **MyCrop App**: A mobile diagnostic tool used by WA agronomists to identify insects, diseases, and nematodes in wheat, barley, canola, and lupin.
