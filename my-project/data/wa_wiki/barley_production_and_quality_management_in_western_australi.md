# Barley Production and Quality Management in Western Australia

## Summary
Barley production in the Western Australian wheatbelt relies on the strategic interaction of genotype, environment, and management (GxExM) to optimize yield and grain quality. Precision agriculture is critical in this system to manage nitrogen levels and variety selection, ensuring malt barley meets strict industry specifications to avoid the financial risk of downgrading to feed grade.

## Key Facts
- **Regional Production:** Barley is cultivated across the WA wheatbelt, with Agzone 2 identified as the largest producing region, followed by Agzone 3.
- **Malt Quality Parameters:** To achieve malt accreditation, grain must meet seven key criteria: germination, variety purity, hectolitre weight, grain protein, screenings, grain color, and skinning.
- **Variety Dynamics:** WA farmers exhibit rapid adoption rates for new varieties; for example, Spartacus has recently surged in popularity, replacing older varieties like Scope and La Trobe.
- **Herbicide Tolerance:** Clearfield (CL) varieties are utilized for their tolerance to Group B ME herbicides, allowing for more effective weed management.
- **Yield Potential:** Feed barley varieties, such as Leebrook, generally exhibit higher yield potential compared to malt-specific varieties.
- **Quality Risks:** Grain protein and screenings are highly sensitive to seasonal conditions and management practices, making them primary drivers of grain reclassification.

## Local Examples
- **Variety Performance:** The Spartacus variety achieved 104% of the site mean yield during the 2018 season.
- **Adoption Trends:** La Trobe was a dominant variety in 2017, accounting for approximately 30% of WA barley production before its decline.
- **Production Zones:** High-intensity production is concentrated in Agzones 2 and 3.
