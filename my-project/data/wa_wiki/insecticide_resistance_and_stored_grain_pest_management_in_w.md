# Insecticide Resistance and Stored Grain Pest Management in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the dynamics of insecticide resistance in stored grain pests, with a primary focus on the rust red flower beetle within Western Australian (WA) cropping systems. It highlights the critical role of spatial monitoring, farmer education, and stringent biosecurity protocols in preventing the establishment of highly resistant strains and exotic pests that threaten grain export markets.

## Key Facts
- **Resistance Levels:** The rust red flower beetle exhibits a resistance frequency of approximately 80% in WA; however, most local cases are "weak" resistance (requiring 20–40 times the normal dose) compared to "strong" resistance (up to 600 times) found in eastern states.
- **Phosphine Mismanagement:** Resistance is often exacerbated by poor storage practices, such as failing to seal silo hatches or incorrectly applying phosphine tablets, which allows larvae and eggs to survive.
- **Spatial Analysis:** WA utilizes an extensive national database of over 80,000 resistance tests integrated with GPS coordinates, enabling precision mapping of resistance patterns relative to climate and latitude.
- **FOSS Card Technology:** To counter complex labeling, the FOSS card provides simplified fumigation instructions and a low-cost copper strip indicator that corrodes in the presence of phosphine, verifying gas exposure.
- **Biosecurity Risks:** The Capra beetle is identified as a critical exotic threat; its introduction could potentially result in the loss of 50% of WA's grain markets due to strict international quarantine standards.
- **Eradication Protocols:** High-intensity interventions for exotic pests include the use of methyl bromide fumigation and airtight "shrink-wrapping" of infested structures to ensure total eradication.

## Local Examples
- **Eastern Wheat Belt:** A 2015 case study where strong resistance was detected on a single farm due to 11 years of phosphine misuse.
- **Scarborough, Perth:** A biosecurity incident involving the accidental import of Capra beetles via household items from Scotland, traced back to a wheat-filled heat bag.
- **FOSS Card:** A practical, low-cost tool developed for WA farmers to ensure correct phosphine dosage (e.g., 100 tablets for a 50-tonne silo).
