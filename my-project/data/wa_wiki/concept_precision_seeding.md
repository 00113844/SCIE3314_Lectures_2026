# No-Till Seeding and Conservation Systems in WA

## Overview
No-till farming in Western Australia is a strategic conservation framework designed to maximize water-use efficiency (WUE) by minimizing soil disturbance and maximizing surface residue retention. In the moisture-limited, Mediterranean climates of the WA wheatbelt, this system is critical for converting limited rainfall directly into grain yield while protecting fragile, degraded soils from erosion and compaction.

## Key Themes
- **Moisture Conservation and WUE**: Tillage is avoided because each soil turn can result in the loss of 15–20 mm of rainfall through evaporation. Retained stubble acts as an insulating blanket that reduces evaporation, prevents soil sealing by dissipating raindrop kinetic energy, and maintains biopores for improved infiltration.
- **Seeding Mechanism Trade-offs**: Growers utilize two primary technologies: Tine (Knife Point) systems, which use tungsten-tipped points to incorporate herbicides and create protected zones for the crop, and Disc systems, which offer superior stubble handling and lower fuel consumption (approx. 2 L/ha compared to 5 L/ha for tines).
- **Precision Compaction Management**: The integration of Controlled Traffic Farming (CTF) via RTK-GPS guidance (accurate to 2 cm) allows farmers to confine all machinery traffic to permanent tramlines. This eliminates soil compaction within the crop growing zone, preserving soil tilth and enhancing root penetration.
- **Strategic Remedial Tillage**: While the baseline is no-till, "tactical" or "remedial" tillage is employed to address specific constraints. This includes deep ripping every 6–8 years to alleviate compaction, moldboard ploughing to bury herbicide-resistant weed banks, and "delving" in duplex soils to bring clay from depth to the surface to increase water-holding capacity.
- **Systemic Integration**: Successful no-till is not a single practice but a combination of five pillars: reduced soil disturbance, surface residue retention, diverse crop rotations, cultural weed control (including Harvest Weed Seed Control - HWSC), and controlled traffic.

## Regional Patterns
There is a near-universal adoption rate (approximately 98%) of no-till systems across WA, reflecting a regional consensus on the necessity of moisture preservation. A distinct pattern exists where tillage is shifted from a routine seasonal activity to a strategic, long-term intervention used specifically for soil remediation or resistant weed management.
