# Adjuvants and Spray Solution Management in Western Australia

## Summary
Adjuvants are chemical additives integrated into pesticide spray solutions to optimize tank compatibility, modify physical spray properties, and enhance the interaction between the active ingredient and the target organism. In the Western Australian context, precise adjuvant selection is critical for managing environmental challenges such as high wind-induced drift and the penetration of waxy cuticles in specific pests.

## Key Facts
- **Functional Objectives**: Adjuvants serve three primary roles: modifying tank interactions (compatibility agents), altering spray properties (drift retardants and viscosity modifiers), and enhancing target interaction (surfactants and oils).
- **Surfactant Chemistry**: Surfactants reduce the surface tension of droplets to prevent "bounce" and improve wetting. Their efficacy is governed by the Hydrophilic-Lipophilic Balance (HLB); an HLB < 10 indicates oil solubility, while HLB > 10 indicates water solubility.
- **Oil-Based Adjuvants**: Oils are utilized to reduce droplet evaporation and enhance penetration through waxy cuticles. However, they increase the risk of phytotoxicity (crop burning) and may interfere with the uptake of water-soluble herbicides like glyphosate.
- **Water Quality Management**: Acidifiers and buffers are employed to counteract alkaline or "hard" water (high calcium content), which can otherwise degrade pesticide efficacy.
- **Nutritional Synergies**: Ammonium sulfate serves a dual purpose as a fertilizer and an adjuvant, facilitating the absorption of systemic herbicides such as 2,4-D, MCPA, and imazethapyr.
- **Ultra-Low Volume (ULV)**: ULV applications (<5 L/ha) utilize petroleum or vegetable oils to minimize the evaporation of small droplets, though this requires stringent drift management.

## Local Examples
- **Trifluralin Application**: A volatile herbicide used in WA that requires immediate soil incorporation via seeder bar tines to prevent loss through volatility.
- **Agral**: A widely used non-ionic surfactant in WA to improve insecticide contact and effectiveness against the waxy cuticles of diamondback moths.
- **GRDC Guidelines**: The Grains Research and Development Corporation provides the primary evidence-based publication for adjuvant selection and application in WA grain systems.
