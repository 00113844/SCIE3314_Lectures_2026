# Holistic No-Till Systems and Soil Management in Western Australia

## Summary
No-till farming in Western Australia is a comprehensive system designed to protect ancient, nutrient-poor, and fragile soils from erosion while maximizing water use efficiency (WUE). By integrating stubble retention, precision seeding, and controlled traffic, these systems minimize soil disturbance to conserve limited moisture and enhance soil organic carbon in a dryland environment.

## Key Facts
- **Soil Stability and Conservation**: No-till systems reduce wind erosion and dust storms by maintaining soil cover, which is critical for the fragile, weathered soils of the WA wheat belt.
- **Moisture Management**: Stubble retention reduces soil surface evaporation and stores summer rainfall, enabling earlier sowing and extending the growing season.
- **Seeder Technology**: 
    - **Tine Seeders**: Preferred for better incorporation of volatile pre-emergent herbicides (e.g., trifluralin) and improved crop safety.
    - **Disc Seeders**: More efficient in stony soils or high-stubble environments, offering lower fuel consumption (approx. 2 L/ha less than tine seeders).
- **Strategic Tillage**: While minimized, tillage is used purposefully for specific interventions:
    - **Mouldboard Ploughing**: Used every ~8 years to bury weed seeds (up to 30 cm) or incorporate lime to treat acidity.
    - **Deep Ripping**: Used to break historical hard pans (up to 80 cm) to improve root penetration.
- **Controlled Traffic Farming (CTF)**: Utilizes RTK-GPS to confine machinery to permanent tracks, preventing soil compaction across the growing area and preserving soil structure.
- **Systemic Risks**: Heavy reliance on herbicides for weed control in no-till systems has led to increased herbicide resistance, necessitating diverse crop rotations and cultural controls.

## Local Examples
- **Woburn**: A region where the transition from pre-seeding tillage to no-till practices transformed degraded, erosion-prone land into productive fields.
- **Water Use Efficiency (WUE)**: Demonstrated capability of achieving 17 kg of grain per mm of rainfall during a low-rainfall season (129 mm).
- **Crop Distribution**: The WA cropping landscape is dominated by wheat (65%), followed by barley (16%) and canola (16%), highlighting a need for increased rotational diversity to manage disease and weeds.
