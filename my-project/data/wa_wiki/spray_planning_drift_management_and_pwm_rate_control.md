# Spray Planning, Drift Management, and PWM Rate Control

## Summary
Effective chemical application in Western Australian broadacre systems requires a hierarchical planning approach, integrating farm-level strategy with precise paddock-level execution. The adoption of Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) technology represents a significant advancement in precision agriculture, allowing for variable rate application and speed flexibility without compromising droplet size or pressure.

## Key Facts
- **Strategic Planning Hierarchy**: Spraying operations must be managed at the farm level (chemical rotation and sensitive area protection) and the paddock level (detailed mixing plans, nozzle selection, and rigorous record-keeping).
- **2,4-D Volatility and Risk**: As a highly volatile hormone disruptor, 2,4-D requires strict adherence to label requirements, specifically the use of coarse spray quality to prevent off-site drift into sensitive broadleaf crops.
- **Drift Mitigation Strategies**: Drift is minimized through the use of Air Induction (AI) nozzles, which produce larger droplets, and the avoidance of non-ionic wetters during summer months.
- **Legal Weather Monitoring**: Compliance requires measuring wind speed at 2 meters above ground and temperature/humidity at 1.25 meters (shaded) at the start, during, and end of the application day.
- **PWM Technology**: Pulse Width Modulation controls flow by switching nozzles on and off at high frequencies (10–100 Hz). This maintains constant pressure and droplet size regardless of travel speed.
- **PWM Duty Cycle**: The output is determined by the duty cycle (the percentage of time the nozzle is open), enabling individual nozzle control to prevent over-application during headland turns.
- **Rate Control Comparison**: Unlike pressure-based systems, where increasing speed requires a fourfold pressure increase (increasing drift risk), PWM allows speed flexibility (e.g., 23 km/h to 6 km/h) while maintaining spray quality.

## Local Examples
- **Sensitive Areas**: Vineyards are highlighted as high-risk targets for 2,4-D drift.
- **Precision Hardware**: Implementation of Case IH AIM Command (alternating nozzles) and John Deere Exact Apply (dual solenoids) for optimized coverage.
