# Impact of Climate Change on Crop Disease Dynamics in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the intersection of shifting climatic patterns and the epidemiology of major crop pathogens in Western Australia. It highlights how temperature increases and altered rainfall distributions influence spore release, pathogen viability, and the overall disease pressure on key grain and pasture crops.

## Key Facts
- **Rhizoctonia Bare Patch:** Disease pressure is exacerbated by low rainfall and slow root growth caused by cold or compacted soils, which increases plant vulnerability.
- **Wheat Rust Dynamics:** Pathogen prevalence is temperature-sensitive; stripe rust prefers cooler conditions (10–15°C), whereas stem rust epidemics are likely to increase with rising temperatures.
- **Pea Black Spot:** A significant constraint on field pea production in WA; however, climate modelling suggests that earlier ascospore release and delayed seasonal rains may reduce disease severity and yield loss (potentially below 10%).
- **Clover Scorch:** This pathogen thrives in cool, wet, and windy conditions (17–24°C); increased temperatures (above 28°C) halt its development, leading to a decline in epidemics across WA since the 1970s.
- **Stubble Persistence:** In WA, blackleg spores can remain viable in crop residues for up to four years, a significantly longer duration than observed in New South Wales.
- **Predictive Modelling:** Weather-based models (e.g., Blackleg Manager) are critical for predicting shifts in spore release timing to optimize sowing dates and adaptive management.

## Local Examples
- **Esperance:** Future climate projections indicate that black spot ascospores will likely be released before May, significantly reducing disease pressure during the sowing window.
- **Regional Spore Timing:** Peak spore release for pea black spot varies by location, occurring mid-season in Geraldton but earlier in Skadden due to rainfall patterns.
- **Projected Shifts:** In Dalwallinu, Meriden, and Weijen, black spot spore release is projected to occur approximately four weeks earlier under future climate scenarios.
