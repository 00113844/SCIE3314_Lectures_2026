# Grain Quality and Protein Management in WA Wheat and Barley

## Summary
Grain quality in Western Australian cereals is determined by a complex G x E x M (Genotype x Environment x Management) interaction, where protein content, screenings, and brightness dictate market classification and pricing. Precision management of nitrogen and water during the grain-filling stage is critical to meeting the strict specifications required for premium malt barley and specialty wheat grades.

## Key Facts
- **Wheat Classification:** WA wheat is graded by protein and hardness into categories including Australian Premium Hard (APH), Australian Hard (AH), Australian Premium White (APW), and Australian Standard White (ASW), with specialty grades like Australian Premium Durum and Australian Noodle Wheat (ANW).
- **Malt Barley Specifications:** Premium Malt 1 barley requires a strict protein window (typically 9–12%) and low screenings (<7% through a 2.2 mm sieve) to ensure optimal brewing performance.
- **Nitrogen Dynamics:** Approximately 75% of shoot nitrogen is remobilized to the grain; while high fertilizer rates increase protein, nitrogen carryover from legumes is more stable and lasts up to two years compared to leachable synthetic nitrogen.
- **Screenings and Plumpness:** High screenings (small grains) are primarily caused by water stress during grain fill, powdery mildew, and early weed competition; they are less common in wheat than in barley.
- **Grain Brightness:** Measured in lux (>59 lux target), brightness is compromised by high humidity, light showers, or mold, which can lead to grain yellowing or graying.
- **Falling Numbers:** This metric assesses dough strength; low falling numbers indicate pre-harvest germination caused by rain, which increases alpha-amylase levels and reduces flour quality.

## Local Examples
- **Regional Constraints:** The WA South Coast is particularly prone to grain brightness issues due to higher humidity and rainfall.
- **Variety Selection:** Barley varieties such as Bodan and Vlaming are noted for producing plumper grains (lower screenings) for a given grain weight.
- **Market Standards:** The use of the 2021 Western Australian Crop Sowing Guide and Grain Trade Australia (GTA) standards for commodity classification.
- **Specialty Grades:** The APWN (Australian Premium White Noodle) classification specifically for WA exports.
