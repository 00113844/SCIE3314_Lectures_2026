# Pesticide Application and Droplet Size Management in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic covers the technical and regulatory requirements for broad-area pesticide application within Western Australian cropping systems. It emphasizes the critical trade-off between droplet coverage and spray drift, highlighting how nozzle selection and droplet size optimization are essential for maximizing efficacy while minimizing environmental impact.

## Key Facts
- **Regulatory Compliance:** Pesticide application in Western Australia requires mandatory training through the OzChem program, focusing on safe handling, storage (including bunding), and application protocols.
- **Droplet Size Metrics:** Spray quality is measured using Volume Median Diameter (VMD), which divides the spray volume into two equal halves, and Number Median Diameter (NMD), which divides the total number of droplets.
- **The Coverage-Drift Trade-off:** Smaller droplets provide significantly higher coverage (e.g., 100 $\mu$m droplets yield ~1.9 million droplets per ml compared to 1,910 for 1 mm droplets) but are highly susceptible to wind drift.
- **Spray Classifications:**
    - **Coarse (>300 $\mu$m):** Low drift; ideal for soil-acting herbicides.
    - **Medium (200–300 $\mu$m):** Versatile balance of coverage and drift control; commonly used for foliar herbicides and fungicides.
    - **Fine (<200 $\mu$m):** High coverage and canopy penetration; preferred for foliar insecticides and fungicides, though drift risk is high.
- **Chemical Interaction:** Contact herbicides require high coverage for efficacy, whereas systemic herbicides are more forgiving as they translocate through the plant tissue.
- **Operator Safety:** The use of tractor cabs with filtered air is the primary technical control for reducing applicator exposure to chemical drift.
