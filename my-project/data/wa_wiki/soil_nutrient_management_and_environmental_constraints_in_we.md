# Soil Nutrient Management and Environmental Constraints in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the chemical and physical constraints of Western Australian soils, focusing on the relationship between soil pH, nutrient availability, and water-use efficiency. It emphasizes the application of precision tools, such as the iLime app and DNA-based disease testing, to optimize input efficiency and manage subsoil acidity and sodicity in no-till systems.

## Key Facts
- **Yield Potential Estimation**: A standard formula for wheat yield potential in WA is: $\text{(Growing season rainfall [April–Oct]} - 110\text{ mm)} \times 20\text{ kg grain/mm}$. Planning typically utilizes decile 5 rainfall data to maintain a realistic 75% yield target.
- **Soil pH and Toxicity**: The optimal soil pH for nutrient availability is 5.5–6.0. Aluminium toxicity becomes a critical constraint when pH drops below 4.7–4.8 (measured in $\text{CaCl}_2$).
- **Phosphorus Buffering Index (PBI)**: Phosphorus availability is heavily influenced by PBI; soils with high PBI (typically clays) lock up more phosphorus, requiring higher critical Colwell P levels (up to 43 mg/kg) compared to low PBI sands (10 mg/kg).
- **Water Availability by Texture**: Sandy soils exhibit high volumetric water availability despite low total capacity, whereas clay soils may hold up to 40% volumetric water, but over 30% of that is typically unavailable to the plant.
- **No-Till Sampling Protocols**: To account for fertilizer banding in no-till systems, sampling should follow a specific ratio—typically 6 inter-row samples to 1 row sample per 6–7 cores—to ensure representative nutrient data.
- **Sodicity vs. Salinity**: Sodic soils (high sodium) are ameliorated using gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4$) to exchange sodium for calcium and improve structure, whereas saline soils require the selection of salt-tolerant cultivars.

## Local Examples
- **Kunderden**: Used as a case study for yield potential; rainfall of 207 mm (April–Oct) results in a potential yield of 1,940 kg/ha.
- **iLime App**: A Department of Agriculture tool used to calculate lime requirements and model long-term pH impacts; data indicates 72% of WA topsoils are below target pH.
- **Predictor B**: A DNA-based diagnostic service from SARDI used to assess soil-borne disease risks for crown rot, Rhizoctonia, and Take-all.
