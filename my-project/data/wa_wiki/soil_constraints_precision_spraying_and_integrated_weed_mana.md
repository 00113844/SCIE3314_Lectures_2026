# Soil Constraints, Precision Spraying, and Integrated Weed Management in WA

## Summary
This topic examines the intersection of soil physical properties, precision application technology, and strategic weed control within Western Australian cropping systems. It emphasizes the non-inert nature of gravel soils, the transition from pressure-based to Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) spraying to manage drift, and the use of Integrated Weed Management (IWM) to deplete the seed banks of herbicide-resistant species.

## Key Facts
- **Gravel Soil Dynamics**: Gravels are not chemically inert; aluminum and ironstone gravels significantly increase the Phosphorus Buffering Index (PBI), often reaching values of 128–130, which restricts phosphorus availability.
- **Strategic Tillage**: In specific sandy gravel soils (e.g., Darkan), moldboarding can increase yields by bringing subsurface clay to the surface, though this should be limited to once every 10 years to prevent the loss of soil organic carbon.
- **PWM Spray Technology**: Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) maintains constant nozzle pressure by pulsing the flow on and off, ensuring a uniform droplet size regardless of vehicle speed, unlike pressure-based systems where increased speed requires higher pressure and results in smaller, drift-prone droplets.
- **PWM Operational Parameters**: To ensure even distribution and avoid "striping," PWM duty cycles should generally be maintained above 60% for medium droplets and 70% for coarse droplets.
- **Ryegrass (*Lolium rigidum*) Management**: A primary constraint in WA, ryegrass is characterized by high phenotypic plasticity and Group 1 herbicide resistance; a "gold standard" for control is the depletion of the seed bank through three consecutive years of effective management.
- **Wild Radish (*Raphanus raphanistrum*) Dynamics**: Radish seeds exhibit staggered germination and higher longevity when buried (e.g., 43% survival at 10 cm after four years vs. 5% on the surface), making surface-based decay strategies less effective than for ryegrass.
- **Harvest Weed Seed Control (HWSC)**: A critical WA innovation that captures weed seeds within the chaff during harvest, providing a non-chemical means of reducing the seed bank.

## Local Examples
- **Esperance Region**: Characterized by porous silica gravels that influence water retention and soil properties.
- **Darkan and Williams Districts**: Areas where strategic tillage in sandy gravel soils has been shown to improve yield by redistributing clay content.
- **Lolium rigidum**: Identified as the most significant weed in Australian cropping due to its adaptability and seed production (up to 45,000 seeds/m²).
