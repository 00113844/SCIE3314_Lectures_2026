# Lupin and Canola Production and Management in Western Australia

## Summary
This page details the agronomic requirements and management strategies for lupins and canola within Western Australian dryland systems. It emphasizes the critical role of biological nitrogen fixation in legumes and the utilization of herbicide-tolerant cultivars to manage weed competition in broadleaf cropping systems.

## Key Facts
- **Lupin Species and Soil Adaptation**: Narrow leaf lupins (developed at UWA) are optimized for acid sands, whereas Albus lupins are better suited for alkaline soils. Sandplain (blue) lupins are now largely considered weeds due to high alkaloid content and anthracnose susceptibility.
- **Biological Nitrogen Fixation**: Lupins require Group G rhizobia inoculants. While rhizobia persist in the soil, re-inoculation is recommended every 4–7 years to counteract genetic mixing with less efficient natural strains.
- **Lupin Nutrient Requirements**: Nitrogen fertilizer is unnecessary; however, phosphorus, potassium, and trace elements are essential. Molybdenum is critical for nitrogen fixation, and manganese deficiency must be managed to prevent seed split and discoloration.
- **Canola Variety Types**: WA exclusively grows spring-type canola. Farmers choose between Open-Pollinated (OP) varieties, which allow seed saving, and Hybrids, which offer increased vigor and yield potential.
- **Canola Herbicide Technology**: Weed management is facilitated by several tolerance traits: Triazine-tolerant (TT) for atrazine use, Clearfield for imidazolinone (effective against brome and barley grass), and Roundup Ready (RR) for glyphosate application.
- **Canola Nutrition**: As a brassica, canola has a high sulfur requirement, typically managed through the application of gypsum or ammonium sulfate.
- **Precision Seeding and Establishment**: Lupin targets are 45–50 plants/m² (100–120 kg/ha), while canola targets 15–30 plants/m² (1–6 kg/ha). Wide row spacing is often employed to allow canopy expansion and improve yields.
- **Weed Control Constraints**: Managing broadleaf weeds in lupins is challenging. Pre-emergent herbicides like trifluralin require soil incorporation within 12 hours, making tine seeders more effective than disc seeders in no-till systems.

## Local Examples
- **UWA Cultivars**: Narrow leaf lupin varieties Uni White and Uni Crop.
- **pH Adaptation**: The Amira lupin cultivar, which allows expansion into slightly higher pH soils.
- **Weed Management**: The use of crop topping with paraquat or glyphosate timed specifically to the "milky dough stage" of ryegrass.
