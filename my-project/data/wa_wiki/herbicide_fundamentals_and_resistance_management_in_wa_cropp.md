# Herbicide Fundamentals and Resistance Management in WA Cropping Systems

## Summary
Herbicides are critical inputs in Western Australian broad-acre farming, serving as the primary technological enabler for no-till cropping systems by replacing mechanical cultivation. Effective management requires a technical understanding of herbicide chemistry, plant physiology, and the mechanisms of weed resistance to maintain yield potential and soil health.

## Key Facts
- **No-Till Integration**: Herbicides substitute destructive tillage, allowing for the conservation of soil organic matter, reduced diesel consumption, and earlier seeding windows.
- **Pre-emergent Dynamics**: These herbicides target seedlings via root hairs and epidermis; their efficacy is heavily dependent on soil moisture for activation and is subject to losses via photodecomposition, volatilization, and leaching.
- **Chemical Properties**: 
    - **Vapor Pressure**: High vapor pressure increases the risk of volatilization (e.g., trifluralin), often requiring soil incorporation.
    - **Water Solubility**: High solubility increases leaching risk during heavy rainfall, while low solubility reduces mobility.
    - **Koc (Organic Carbon Partition Coefficient)**: Determines the strength of binding to organic matter; high Koc herbicides may bind to surface stubble, reducing soil penetration.
- **Post-emergent Constraints**: Uptake is limited by the leaf cuticle and can be severely reduced by plant stress (drought, frost, or waterlogging) which closes stomata, or by surface dust that inactivates active ingredients like glyphosate.
- **Resistance Mechanisms**: 
    - **Target Site Resistance**: Genetic modification of the target enzyme, often resulting in high levels of resistance.
    - **Non-Target Site Resistance**: Metabolic detoxification or sequestration of the herbicide before it reaches the target site.
- **Selectivity**: Crop safety is often achieved through the crop's ability to detoxify the chemical (e.g., wheat and barley detoxifying Group A herbicides) or through positional safety (applying herbicides between seed rows).

## Local Examples
- **Pyroxasulfone (Sakura)**: A pre-emergent herbicide used in WA that presents specific carryover risks to subsequent rotational crops like canola or oats, particularly under dry soil conditions.
- **Trifluralin**: Managed in wheat systems using positional safety to avoid phytotoxicity while controlling weeds.
- **Diclofop-methyl**: A Group A herbicide used selectively for grass control in wheat and barley.
- **Fluazifop**: Utilized for grass control in broadleaf crops such as canola and lupins.
