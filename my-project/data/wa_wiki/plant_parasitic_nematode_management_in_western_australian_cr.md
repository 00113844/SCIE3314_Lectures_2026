# Plant Parasitic Nematode Management in Western Australian Cropping Systems

## Summary
Plant parasitic nematodes, specifically root lesion nematodes (*Pratylenchus* spp.) and cereal cyst nematodes (*Heterodera* spp.), represent significant subterranean constraints to crop productivity in Western Australia. Management in broadacre systems relies on integrated strategies focusing on genetic resistance, tolerance, and strategic crop rotation, as chemical controls are generally impractical.

## Key Facts
- **Prevalence:** Approximately 60% of cropping paddocks in Western Australia are infested with one or more species of root lesion nematode, with *Pratylenchus neglectus* being the most widespread.
- **Pathology:** Nematodes utilize a stylet to puncture plant cells and feed on root tissues, reducing root hair density and impairing the plant's ability to uptake water and nutrients.
- **Diagnostic Challenges:** Above-ground symptoms—such as chlorotic patches, yellowing, and general stunting—are non-specific and frequently mimic drought stress or nutrient deficiencies.
- **Management Philosophy:** Control focuses on the reduction of population densities rather than complete eradication.
- **Resistance vs. Tolerance:** Resistance is defined by a low nematode multiplication rate (<1), whereas tolerance refers to the ability of a cultivar to maintain growth and yield despite high nematode populations.
- **Host Specificity:** Cereal cyst nematodes are limited to grass hosts, making rotation with non-grass crops (e.g., legumes) an effective management tool.

## Local Examples
- **Rotation Efficacy:** Trials from 2005-2006 demonstrated that rotating with resistant crops could reduce *P. neglectus* levels from 11.3 to 0.5 nematodes per gram of soil.
- **Cultivar Responses:** Yitpi wheat is noted for being both resistant and tolerant to cereal cyst nematode, while Jans wheat is characterized as susceptible and intolerant.
- **Oat Varieties:** Potteroo oats exhibit both resistance and tolerance to nematode infestation.

Wheat, Canola, Barley, Oats, Lupins, Field Peas, Fava Beans
