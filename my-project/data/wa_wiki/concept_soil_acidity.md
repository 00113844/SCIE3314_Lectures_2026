# Precision Management of Soil Acidity in Western Australia

## Overview
Soil acidity represents a primary environmental constraint within Western Australian cropping systems, manifesting as nutrient deficiencies and aluminum toxicity that severely limit yield potential. Precision management focuses on the strategic application of lime guided by decision-support tools to optimize the economic return on investment while mitigating the physiological impacts of low pH.

## Key Themes
- **Critical pH Thresholds and Toxicity**: Agronomic productivity is compromised when surface soils drop below pH 5.5, limiting nutrient availability. More critically, subsoil pH below 4.8 triggers the release of toxic aluminum ($\text{Al}^{3+}$), which inhibits root elongation and restricts the plant's ability to access deep soil moisture.
- **Economic Impact of Acidification**: Soil acidity is a significant systemic cost to the WA agricultural industry, with estimated annual losses in yield income ranging between $500 million and $1,500 million.
- **Predictive Decision Support**: The iLime app (utilizing the Oplime engine) allows for the simulation of pH trajectories and profitability over 20-year horizons. This tool demonstrates high predictive accuracy, with an R-squared value of 0.8 when validated against long-term field trials.
- **Biogeochemical Drivers**: Acidification is primarily driven by the export of basic cations through harvested grains and the leaching of nitrates, both of which release protons into the soil profile.
- **Agronomic Trade-offs of Liming**: While neutralizing acidity, lime application can induce secondary constraints, including manganese (Mn) deficiency—requiring foliar correction—and an increased prevalence of take-all disease and snail populations.
- **Deep Soil Re-engineering**: To address concurrent acidity and compaction constraints, experimental interventions involving the incorporation of lime and organic matter to depths of 70–90 cm have been trialed, showing potential to significantly increase yields.

## Regional Patterns
Data from the Avon River Basin indicates a positive trend in soil health, with the proportion of topsoil below pH 5.5 decreasing from 72% to 46%. Conversely, the Eastern Wheatbelt is characterized by high soil variability, necessitating zoned management and rigorous ground-truthing of digital lime recommendations.
