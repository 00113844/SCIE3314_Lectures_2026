# Malting Barley Quality and Agronomic Management in Western Australia

## Summary
This topic examines the agronomic drivers of grain quality for malting barley in Western Australia, focusing on the critical parameters of protein content, screenings, and grain brightness. Precision management of nitrogen, sowing dates, and crop rotations is essential to meet strict industry specifications and avoid quality penalties.

## Key Facts
- **Grain Protein Targets:** The optimal window for malting barley is 9.5% to 12.5%; protein levels outside this range negatively impact alcohol yield, filtration, and beer head stability.
- **Screenings Management:** Malt 1 barley requires screenings of less than 7% (through a 2.2 mm screen). High screenings are primarily driven by moisture stress during grain filling, frost damage, and disease.
- **Nitrogen Dynamics:** Total nitrogen (N) application is the primary driver of grain protein. Non-legume rotations (e.g., canola or wheat) are preferred prior to barley to prevent protein overshoot caused by high background soil N.
- **Environmental Impacts:** Frost during the milky to soft dough stage can increase screenings up to 70%, a risk exacerbated by high stubble which reduces soil heat radiation.
- **Disease Control:** Fungicide applications can significantly reduce screenings (e.g., from 45% to 20%) by mitigating the impact of net blotch, scald, and powdery mildew.
- **Precision Decision Support:** Tools such as Yield Prophet provide higher accuracy for nitrogen decision-making compared to static estimation rules by integrating actual soil data and rainfall.

## Local Examples
- **Varietal Performance:** Vlaming exhibits lower screenings than Bodan at similar grain weights; Bass and Sterling tend to produce higher protein than Flinders or Granger.
- **Regional Row Spacing:** Coastal WA typically utilizes narrower rows (22 cm), whereas the drier eastern wheat belt employs wider rows (30 cm) for moisture conservation.
- **Soil N Estimation:** The Department of Agriculture suggests a rule of thumb of 40 kg N/ha in medium rainfall zones following a successful legume rotation.
