# Grain Screenings and Plumpness Management in WA Barley and Wheat

## Summary
Grain screenings are a critical quality metric in Western Australian cereal production, measuring the percentage of grain that passes through a 2 mm slotted sieve to determine plumpness. In precision agriculture, managing screenings is essential for maximizing the malting potential of barley and the market grade of wheat, as plumpness directly influences water absorption rates and overall grain weight.

## Key Facts
- **Quality Thresholds:** Target screenings are generally less than 10% for barley and less than 5% for wheat.
- **Grain Weight Correlation:** There is a strong inverse correlation between screenings and grain weight; lower screenings indicate plumper grains (typically 30–40 mg per grain).
- **Environmental Influence:** A "soft finish"—characterized by ample moisture availability during the grain-filling stage—is the primary driver for reducing screenings and increasing yield.
- **Nitrogen Interaction:** While nitrogen is essential for yield, excessive application during dry spring conditions can significantly increase screenings.
- **Pathogen Impact:** Powdery mildew is a dominant disease affecting grain plumpness; varieties with Moderate Resistance to Moderate Susceptibility (MR-MS) typically exhibit lower screenings than Susceptible (S) varieties.
- **Agronomic Drivers:** Weed competition, particularly from ryegrass, increases screenings, whereas seed rate and row spacing have negligible effects on grain plumpness.

## Local Examples
- **Varietal Performance:** Lambing and Bodan varieties typically exhibit lower screenings compared to Bullock or Flagship (which can reach up to 28%).
- **Site-Specific Nitrogen Response:** In dry spring conditions at Kalingari and Gibson, high nitrogen levels increased screenings, whereas sites like Beverley and Jayka with average rainfall maintained screenings below critical levels.
- **Irrigation Impact:** Department of Agriculture trials demonstrated that applying 25 mm of irrigation 7 days after awn emergence reduced barley screenings from 80% to 15%.
