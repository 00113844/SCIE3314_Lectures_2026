# Introduction to Agricultural Pests and Biosecurity in Western Australia

## Summary
Agricultural pests are defined anthropocentrically as organisms that negatively impact human interests, with their status often depending on geographic location and specific environmental contexts. In Western Australia, the management of these pests relies heavily on strict quarantine protocols and an understanding of pest biology to mitigate yield losses and maintain biosecurity.

## Key Facts
- **Anthropocentric Nature:** A species is classified as a "pest" based on its impact on human production or infrastructure; for example, honeybees are beneficial pollinators but can be considered pests in specific sensitive environments.
- **Biosecurity and Quarantine:** Due to Australia's geographic isolation, quarantine is the primary defense against the introduction of devastating foreign pests and diseases, such as citrus canker.
- **Arthropod Dominance:** Insects and related arthropods (such as mites and ticks) constitute approximately 90% of terrestrial agricultural pests.
- **Developmental Biology:** Pest control strategies are dictated by insect growth types: ametabolous (no change), hemimetabolous (partial change), and holometabolous (complete metamorphosis).
- **Vector Potential:** Certain specialist pests, such as the Russian wheat aphid, cause damage not only through sap-sucking but by acting as vectors for plant viruses.

## Local Examples
- **Red-legged Earth Mites:** Impact pastures and canola in Australia, causing "silvering" of leaves by extracting cell contents, particularly during the seedling stage.
- **Slaters (Woodlice):** Land-based crustaceans that attack emergent seedlings in southern Western Australia.
- **Snails:** Non-native Mediterranean species that contaminate wheat and canola harvests with shells.
- **Diamondback Moth:** A significant pest of canola and brassicas in Australia known for developing resistance to various control measures.
