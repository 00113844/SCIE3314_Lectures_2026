# Technical components of a paddock-scale cropping system

The unit's central "holistic project" framing: one paddock treated as a system, broken into
distinct technical components an agronomist must manage together, not in isolation.

## Key points taught (L01)
- Soil sub-system: chemistry (pH, nutrient availability, salinity, CEC), physics (texture,
  structure, subsoil compaction, water-holding capacity), biology (organic matter, microbiome,
  mycorrhizae, soilborne pathogens)
- Crop production sub-system: seed/variety choice; nutrition (fertiliser type, rate, timing);
  crop protection — herbicides (weeds), fungicides (disease), insecticides (pests), each with its
  own mode-of-action/resistance logic
- Machinery and operations; management layer (records, financial planning, risk)
- Components are interdependent: a soil-physics/biology choice (stubble retention) has
  soil-chemistry consequences (nitrogen tie-up) and crop-protection consequences (disease
  carryover)

## Grounding note
Soil chemistry and physics are well covered in `wa_wiki` (agronomic planning, soil-water, yield-
potential docs). Soil biology specifically (microbiome, mycorrhizae) is thin — mostly folded into
crop-rotation and no-till documents rather than treated as its own topic. Don't over-claim wiki
grounding for the biology third of this slide.
