# Conservation agriculture, no-till, and strategic tillage

WA's shift from intensive cultivation to no-till (late 1980s-2000s), the three principles of
conservation agriculture, why some tillage is still strategically reintroduced, and the seeding
machinery choice (tine vs disc) that makes no-till workable.

## Key points taught (L01)
- Shift from cultivation to no-till; erosion and moisture loss under traditional cultivation;
  glyphosate's role in enabling no-till
- Three principles: minimum soil disturbance, permanent soil cover, crop rotation and diversity —
  reinforce each other as a system
- Problems from persistent no-till (compaction, water repellence); deep ripping and mouldboard
  ploughing as strategic, infrequent interventions
- Seeding system components (opener, delivery, press wheels, cart); tine openers (robust, more
  disturbance) vs disc openers (low disturbance, higher cost)
