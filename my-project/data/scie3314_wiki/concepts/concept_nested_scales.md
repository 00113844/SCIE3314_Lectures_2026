# Nested scales — soil, paddock, farm, market

A cropping system nests inside larger systems and contains smaller ones. Reasoning well in this
unit means being explicit about which scale a question is asked at.

## Key points taught (L01)
- Soil scale: chemistry, physics, biology below the paddock (see
  [[concept_paddock_technical_components]])
- Paddock scale: the technical components operating together across one season
- Farm scale: a business system — multiple paddocks, rotations, machinery capacity, labour, cash
  flow, risk, across years
- Market scale: prices, export demand, government policy, global food security
- Scales are nested, not separate — worked example: subsoil acidity (soil) → yield-potential
  problem (paddock) → lime-investment decision (farm) → shaped by grain prices (market)

## Grounding note
`wa_wiki` covers the market end well (global grain production/sustainable intensification docs)
but has little dedicated farm-business content (machinery capacity, labour, cash flow as a
system) — that material leans more on `crop_gross_margin_analysis_and_financial_planning_in_western`
and `gross_margin_analysis_and_profitability_in_wa_cropping_syste`, which are financial-analysis
docs rather than farm-as-system docs. Expect L10 (Farm Financial and Production Analysis and Risk
Management) and L13 (Process Management and Grain Logistics) to be the real anchors for the
farm-business scale, and L14 (Global Food Markets) for the market scale.
