# Weed and herbicide management

Herbicides underpin no-till and early sowing in Australian rainfed cropping, but they are one tool in integrated weed management. Resistance, crop injury, residues and off-target movement make repeated reliance on a single tactic unsafe.

## Classifications

- **Selective** products control specified weeds while being safe in the labelled crop when used correctly; **non-selective** products are broad spectrum.
- **Pre-plant** occurs before seeding; **pre-emergent/soil-applied** herbicides act around weed germination; **post-emergent** products act on established foliage and may or may not have residual soil activity.

Soil-applied herbicides depend on water, soil binding, degradation, volatility, placement and crop avoidance of the treated zone. In no-till, they are commonly applied around sowing and incorporated by rainfall or light soil movement.

Foliar uptake is best on young, actively growing, unstressed weeds. Leaf angle, hairs, wax, droplet retention and weather influence performance.

## Resistance stewardship

Rotate and mix effective modes of action, maintain non-herbicide tactics, prevent survivors from setting seed and retain accurate records. The lectures cover groups including ACCase, ALS, dinitroanilines, auxin mimics, PSII inhibitors, EPSPS inhibition and pyridiniums. Always check current Australian classifications, registrations and labels.
