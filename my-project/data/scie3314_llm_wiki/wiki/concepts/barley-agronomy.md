# Barley agronomy and grain quality

Barley is marketed as malt or feed. Malt acceptance depends on an accredited variety and meeting specifications such as moisture, protein, screenings, test weight, germination, purity, colour and skinning.

## The malting decision

Compare the likely malt premium with relative yield, disease and agronomic fit, and the chance of meeting grade. See [[malt-vs-feed-barley]].

## Nitrogen, protein and water

Cereals take up much nitrogen before flowering, especially through stem elongation, then remobilise it into grain. Nitrogen rate and site are primary controls of barley protein; variety and split timing are secondary. Both very low and very high protein are problematic for malt. Match N to realistic yield, water and quality targets, considering soil organic carbon and rotation history.

## Screenings and brightness

Screenings describe small grain and reflect plumpness. They respond to spring water, nitrogen relative to water, soil, variety, disease, insects, sowing time, crop density and weeds. Extra N cannot produce plump grain without sufficient water.

Light rain or high humidity can yellow grain; heavier rain near maturity can cause greying and mould risk. Variety and location influence brightness, but timely harvest and good crop management reduce avoidable risk.

## Practical package

Select a market-accepted and disease-suitable variety; use a soil that holds spring moisture; sow at the appropriate early opportunity; match N to yield and protein; establish an adequate crop; manage foliar disease, aphids and weeds; and evaluate quality at harvest.
