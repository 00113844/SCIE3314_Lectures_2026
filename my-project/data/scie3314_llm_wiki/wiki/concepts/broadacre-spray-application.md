# Broadacre spray application

The goal is uniform, label-compliant product delivery to the target while minimising drift, waste, crop damage and exposure. Safety and legal requirements are part of the technical task.

## Droplets and nozzles

Volume median diameter (VMD) describes droplet size. Coarse droplets reduce drift but may compromise coverage; fine droplets improve coverage but have higher drift risk. Select the label-required spray quality.

Hydraulic nozzles are common in broadacre work. Flat fan, deflector, cone, pre-orifice and air-induction designs differ in pattern and droplet spectrum. Flow changes with the square root of pressure: doubling flow requires about four times the pressure. Pressure changes can therefore alter both rate and drift risk; select nozzle capacity rather than using pressure as the only control.

## Conditions and calibration

Avoid adverse heat, low humidity, unsuitable wind and inversions; the lecture guide uses temperature below about 28°C, Delta T below about 8 and wind around 3–15 km/h, subject always to the product label.

Measure actual nozzle output, speed under paddock conditions and swath width. The standard relationship is:

> **Application rate (L/ha) = output (L/min) × 600 ÷ [speed (km/h) × swath width (m)]**

Recalibrate after changes in nozzles, pressure, speed, spray mix or equipment condition.

## Modern control

Pressure-based controllers, dual or variable-rate nozzles and pulse-width modulation can change flow with travel speed. PWM can retain more consistent pressure and droplet size over a wider operating range and enable individual-nozzle control, but it still needs correct nozzle selection and operator understanding.
