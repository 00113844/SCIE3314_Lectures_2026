# Lupin production

Lupins are Fabaceae legumes with an important role in WA sandplain systems. Narrow-leafed lupin is the main commercial type and is adapted to acid, infertile sands. Albus and yellow lupins differ in soil adaptation and disease response, so type must match site and market.

## Establishment and nutrition

The lecture guide is approximately 40–45 plants/m², with seed placed about 3–5 cm deep. Very shallow or deep sowing changes establishment and disease risk. Use appropriate rhizobial inoculation and supply phosphorus, potassium and required trace elements; nitrogen is largely supplied through fixation but a small starter supply may sometimes be relevant.

## Weed and disease management

Weeds can be difficult and expensive in lupins. Triazine tolerance provides useful options, but resistance status, crop competition, residual products and label constraints still govern the program. Crop topping can be a seed-set management tool in suitable situations.

Key diseases include Pleiochaeta root rot, Rhizoctonia problems, Eradu patch and anthracnose. Rotation and crop type help in some cases, but broad-host pathogens limit rotation alone. Anthracnose is seed-borne: certified seed and suitable seed treatment are important.

## Rotation role

Lupins offer a disease break, alternative weed tactics, nitrogen fixation, useful residue, an operationally spread workload and a protein grain option. The nitrogen benefit to a following cereal is real but uncertain in amount after grazing, erosion, leaching and immobilisation; budget it rather than assume a fixed credit.
