# Paddock planning, soil and crop monitoring

## Build a usable history

Keep records of soil analyses, rainfall, crop stresses, fertiliser treatments, planting, plant-protection applications and machinery operations. This history supports diagnosis, resistance stewardship and later economic evaluation.

Before the season, assess crop profitability and risk, soil capability, variety fit, prior crop and stubble, herbicide history, fertility and disease/nematode risk.

## Sampling and soil constraints

Avoid margins and unrepresentative patches; sample soil types or management zones separately and record GPS locations. Know soil texture and consider row versus inter-row sampling where previous fertiliser bands may distort a result.

Key subsoil constraints include compaction, sodicity, salinity, acidity/alkalinity, boron toxicity and water repellence. Responses can include controlled traffic and ripping, gypsum, crop choice, liming and clay or wetting-agent treatments—but only after diagnosis of the particular constraint.

## Interpreting tests

- pH affects nutrient availability. The lecture guide is to maintain surface pH around 5.0–5.5 and to use layered liming plans.
- Interpret phosphorus with the test method, PBI, pH, soil type and rainfall near sowing in mind.
- Assess nitrogen, sulphur, potassium, organic carbon, EC and exchangeable cations in the context of crop demand, storage and loss pathways.
- Use plant tissue tests to confirm suspected trace-element issues where appropriate.

## Establishment and residue

Retained stubble protects soil from erosion and contains nutrients that can be lost through burning. Check seed health and calculate seed rate from target plants/m², thousand-seed weight and expected establishment. Match sowing date, row spacing and crop density to flowering target, disease risk, soil water and crop competition.
