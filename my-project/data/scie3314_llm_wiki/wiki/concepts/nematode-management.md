# Nematode management in cereal cropping

Plant-parasitic nematodes use a stylet to feed on roots. In broadacre systems the objective is management, not eradication: identify and quantify the species, understand host range, then use a rotation and variety strategy.

## Root lesion nematodes

*Pratylenchus* species are migratory root parasites. They can cause shallow, discoloured roots, poor feeder roots, stunting, chlorosis and patchy growth, but the same symptoms occur with other disorders and different RLN species have different host ranges.

Diagnosis, species-specific crop choice, weed control, resistance/tolerance, nutrition and suitable sowing are the main tools. A rotation that reduces *P. neglectus* may not work for *P. penetrans*, so testing is indispensable.

## Cereal cyst nematode

CCN (*Heterodera avenae*) attacks grasses including cereals and grass weeds. Yellowing, stunting, reduced tillering and distinctive root knotting or cysts make it easier to recognise than RLN. Use non-host crops, resistant and/or tolerant cultivars, grass-weed control and sound nutrition.

## Resistance and tolerance

Tolerance protects the present crop's yield; resistance reduces nematode reproduction. Use both traits in the rotation rather than accepting a tolerant-but-susceptible crop that leaves a larger future problem.
