# Cropping systems and the decision frame

Agronomy is a whole-system activity: productive crops must also be profitable, resilient and compatible with long-term soil condition. The course frames WA cropping around climate change, erosion and conservation farming, rising inputs, herbicide resistance and rotations that work economically as well as biologically.

## G × E × M

Yield and quality arise from the interaction of **genotype**, **environment** and **management**.

- Genotype includes maturity, disease resistance, quality traits and herbicide tolerance.
- Environment includes rainfall, temperature, soil, stored water, pest pressure and constraints.
- Management includes rotation, sowing date and density, nutrition, machinery and crop protection.

No factor can be chosen in isolation. For example, a variety or nitrogen rate suited to one soil and season may be poor in another.

## Decision rule

Use the whole rotation and paddock history to set realistic yield and quality targets, select an agronomy package, monitor outcomes, and feed the result back into next season's plan.
