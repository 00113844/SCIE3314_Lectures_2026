# Precision agriculture

Precision agriculture uses temporal, spatial and individual plant/animal data to improve resource-use efficiency, productivity, quality, profitability and sustainability. It manages measured variation rather than treating every hectare as identical.

## Where it adds value

Site-specific management is most promising when variation is both large and spatially coherent. A paddock with stable low- and high-yield zones presents more opportunity than one with the same average yield but random variation.

## Data layers

Useful layers include calibrated yield data, biomass and vegetation indices, weed/pest/disease maps, soil apparent electrical conductivity, soil depth, elevation, slope and targeted soil samples. Reflectance indices show changes in chlorophyll and vegetation cover, but they do not diagnose why a pattern occurs.

## From map to decision

Put layers on a common grid, define potential management zones and ground-truth their causes. Production maps can support nutrient replacement calculations; unexplained low-yield areas need further diagnosis before applying a prescription.

Variable-rate technology can change rates of fertiliser, lime, gypsum or herbicides. It is data-intensive, and the economic value of a better decision must exceed the cost of data, analysis and equipment.
