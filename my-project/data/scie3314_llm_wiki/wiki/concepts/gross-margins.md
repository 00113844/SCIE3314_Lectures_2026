# Farm economics and gross margins

An enterprise gross margin compares the contribution of different enterprises to farm profit before overheads:

> **Gross margin = annual gross income − variable costs**

It is a planning comparison, not an exact forecast. Use current local assumptions; a roughly correct estimate with realistic inputs is more useful than false precision.

## What belongs in the calculation

Variable costs are directly caused by the crop: land and machinery operations, seed, fertiliser, crop-protection products and application, casual labour, irrigation where relevant, and harvest, storage and freight.

Permanent labour, depreciation, accountancy, rates and taxes, interest, general insurance, rent and family drawings are usually treated as overheads rather than crop-specific variable costs.

## Use it with risk and rotation

Calculate the break-even yield needed to cover variable costs. Then test price, yield, fertiliser and freight assumptions. Do not assess a crop only by its single-year margin: a break crop can alter weeds, disease, residual nitrogen, water and later cereal yield.
