# Diagnosing crop problems

Above-ground symptoms rarely prove a single cause. Start with a whole-paddock pattern, then compare healthy and affected plants, examine roots, check history and confirm with appropriate tests.

## Diagnostic sequence

1. Do not jump to conclusions.
2. Map the pattern: patch, headland, low area, windrow or soil zone.
3. Compare good and poor plants; inspect the whole plant, especially roots.
4. Record symptom type—lesions, pustules, blotches, necrosis, chlorosis or abnormal growth—and whether old or young tissue is affected.
5. Check weather, fertiliser, spray, crop and rotation records.
6. Verify with soil, tissue, seed, grain, laboratory or DNA-based tests.
7. Allow for interacting causes.

## Tissue testing

For cereals, sampling around tillering can leave time for a corrective treatment. Use whole shoots while plants are small; otherwise use defined tissue positions such as the youngest fully expanded leaf for trace elements. Canola is commonly sampled at rosette stage using a young, fully developed middle leaf. Concentrations only have meaning when interpreted for the correct crop, stage, tissue and method.
